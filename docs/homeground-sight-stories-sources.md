# Sight stories: sources

The writing in `lib/sightStories.ts` keeps to facts that do not date quickly,
such as history, sizes and names. Hours, prices and booking steps belong to
the reservation rules and the Travel Advice guides. Each fact below names the
source it was checked against.

Judgments are Homeground's own and are written as advice. Examples: when to
arrive, what to skip, and how long to give a sight.

Checked on 2026-10-04.

## Temple of Heaven

| Fact | Source |

| Fact | Source |
| --- | --- |

## Beijing: open points

- Badaling's opening year: 1953 or 1958 depending on the source; written as “in the 1950s”.
- Who built Badaling's hollow towers (Tan Lun or Qi Jiguang) is disputed; both are named together.
- Hall of Prayer roof change: 1751 or 1752; written as “the 1750s”.
- Seven-Star Stones (Temple of Heaven) were left out: Beijing Daily says the present stones are a park restoration and the originals are lost.
- Oregon-pine timber in the 1890s rebuild of the Hall of Prayer was left out: the park's own page says the 28 pillars are nanmu.
- Why the Houmuwu Ding stayed on the mainland in 1949 is disputed (weight, or staff reluctance); left out.
- Suzhou Street rebuild year: 1988 (Wikipedia) or 1990 (Baidu); written as “a modern rebuild”.
- Korean names: 부흥의 길, 사양방존 and 후모무정 now have market hits (review round 1); 설창용 and 청안방 have none and are glossed or shown with hanja.
- (v2) Mutianyu's build year: 1368 (operator) against 1404 for the Zhengguantai (other sources) and 明初 (Huairou government); written as “early in the Ming” in all three languages.
- (v2) Long Corridor per-novel painting counts: unsourced, removed.
- (v2) The Yaoyue Gate magnolia: about 180 years old by the park's record, so not a Qianlong-era planting; the story says only that it is the one survivor of 1860.
- (v2) The Temple of Heaven's “round hall” dates from 1545 (as 大享殿); the story ties only the Circular Mound to 1530 and describes the harvest prayer without a start date.
