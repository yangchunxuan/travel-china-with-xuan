# China Travel Guide: Start With the Decision That Changes Your Trip

China is too large, and the practical systems are too connected, for one useful travel guide to be a giant list of sights. This page is the national starting point. Use it to identify the decision in front of you, open the guide that owns that decision, and return here when the next part of the trip becomes clear.

If this is your first trip, begin with four facts: the nights you actually have, the international gateways you can use, the places that matter most, and the needs of the slowest traveller in the group. Then move through **inspiration → choice → validation → human help**. Entry rules, train schedules, attraction tickets and hotel conditions can change; the relevant guide tells you what must be checked again close to travel.

## 1. Choose the question before collecting answers

Most planning mistakes begin when several different questions are treated as one. A beautiful place does not prove that it fits the route. A fast train does not prove that the transfer is short. A room listing does not prove that the hotel is a good base. Start with the row that best describes where you are now.

| Your situation | First place to go | What you should decide there |
|---|---|---|
| “I need ideas.” | [Explore China](/explore/) | Which places or experiences deserve a role in this trip |
| “I want to understand what I will see.” | [Culture and context](/culture/) | Which history, living practice, food or art guide gives the place meaning |
| “I have a wish list.” | [Plan Your Trip](/plan/) | Nights, city roles, order, gateways, bases and pace |
| “I need to know whether I can enter.” | [Entry and practical guides](/essentials/) | Which official rule applies to the passport, purpose and route |
| “I am comparing trains, flights or transfers.” | [Getting Around](/transport/) | The complete door-to-door movement, not only scheduled travel time |
| “I do not know where to stay.” | [Where to Stay](/stay/) | The base that supports repeated days before comparing properties |
| “My dates may be difficult.” | [When to Go](/when-to-go/) | Whether weather, public holidays or seasonal access changes the plan |

This routing is deliberate. Homeground does not publish another page for every city × month × trip length × traveller combination. A narrower page exists only when the combination creates a distinct decision that the parent guide cannot resolve.

## 2. Confirm the entry path before fixing the route

Entry is a gate, not a paragraph to skim after buying transport. Match the official rule to the passport, purpose of travel, points of arrival and departure, and every transit on the way. A rule that works for one nationality or flight path does not automatically work for another. Homeground explains public rules but does not decide individual eligibility or submit visa applications.

Use [China Essentials](/essentials/) to find the relevant passport or transit owner. Read the source-review date, then verify against the National Immigration Administration, the responsible Chinese embassy or consulate, and the carrier before departure. If any part remains uncertain, keep the route and non-refundable bookings flexible until the responsible authority confirms it.

The same section covers the practical layer that can consume the first hours: payment setup, mobile connectivity, booking identity and accommodation registration. Solve these before attaching a scarce timed attraction to the arrival day. The [arrival-day decision guide](/guides/china-arrival-day-booked-anchor-or-flexible-block/) explains when the first day should contain only replaceable activities.

## 3. Give every destination a job

Inspiration becomes a route only after each place has a reason to stay. Open [Explore China](/explore/) and mark each candidate as one of five roles:

- **gateway:** it makes international arrival or departure work;
- **anchor:** it contains a top-priority experience that justifies several nights;
- **contrast:** it adds a landscape, history, food or pace not already supplied elsewhere;
- **recovery base:** it reduces movement and supports a slower part of the trip;
- **optional extension:** it is valuable only if the transfer and hotel change leave enough usable time.

Two places with the same role are the first candidates for a cut. A famous name is not automatically an anchor, and a nearby place is not automatically an easy addition. Read the relevant destination Hub for neighbourhoods, usable bases and the onward route; use attraction guides only after the city has earned its place.

This page does not reproduce city itineraries. Named route-order questions belong to their own owners, such as [Beijing, Xi’an and Chengdu route order](/guides/beijing-xian-chengdu-route-order/) or [Shanghai, Suzhou, Hangzhou and Nanjing route order](/guides/shanghai-suzhou-hangzhou-nanjing-route-order/).

## 4. Turn nights into a route—not a list of dates

Open the [China itinerary and first-trip Hub](/plan/) when you have dates or candidate places. Start with nights, because a seven-night trip creates eight calendar dates but not eight full sightseeing days. Arrival and departure windows, immigration, station access, baggage, hotel check-in and fatigue determine how much of the edge days remains.

Next, test direction. Different international gateways can remove backtracking; [open-jaw flight planning](/guides/china-open-jaw-flights-route-planning/) owns that calculation. A single base may protect energy, while advancing bases may protect distance; [hub-and-spoke versus multi-base planning](/guides/china-hub-and-spoke-or-multi-base-route/) owns that choice.

Finally, test pressure with the published guide [Is Your China Itinerary Too Rushed?](/guides/is-your-china-itinerary-too-rushed/). This Hub does not run an automated route calculator. It must never promise an instant score, silently collect route inputs or create generated itinerary URLs.

## 5. Count the whole movement between two hotel rooms

A timetable shows only one component of a transfer. The route must also absorb packing, checkout, travel to the correct terminal or station, security and waiting, the scheduled journey, arrival circulation, the last-mile ride and the next check-in. A short rail segment can therefore break up most of a day; a longer ride may be manageable when both endpoints are simple.

Use [Getting Around](/transport/) for the exact station, airport or city pair. If rail is new to you, begin with the [first-time China high-speed train guide](/guides/china-high-speed-train-first-time-guide/). If an overnight train appears to “save a day,” compare sleep, wash-up, luggage and arrival readiness in [night train or daytime high-speed rail](/guides/china-night-train-or-daytime-high-speed-rail/). For one difficult terminal leg, [private transfer or public transport](/guides/china-private-transfer-or-public-transport/) compares luggage, mobility, time risk and recovery rather than assuming one mode is always better.

Do not copy a current timetable or fare from a Hub. Verify the exact date, station name, operating carrier and booking channel when sales open or the travel date approaches.

## 6. Choose a base before choosing a room

Accommodation is route infrastructure. A lower nightly rate can be poor value if every morning begins with an extra crossing, the advertised metro stop is the wrong entrance, or a late arrival depends on an unstaffed check-in. Open [Where to Stay](/stay/) and choose the area by the journeys that repeat, then compare properties.

For a national test, read [How Close Should Your Hotel Be to the Metro in China?](/guides/china-hotel-near-metro/). Check the precise entrance, street-level walking route, lifts or steps, after-hours access, curb-to-reception path, passport acceptance and the hotel’s own confirmation. Travellers using wheelchairs should verify the full chain in [wheelchair-accessible China route planning](/guides/wheelchair-accessible-china-route-planning/); one ramp does not establish an accessible trip.

A hotel change is justified when it removes repeated long journeys or protects a critical departure. It is not automatically worthwhile because two districts look far apart on a map. The cost includes repacking, checkout, luggage custody, the transfer and settling in again.

## 7. Put holidays and fixed bookings onto the same calendar

Some dates change national transport demand and attraction pressure. Use the [China public-holiday travel calendar](/guides/china-public-holidays-travel-calendar/) for official holiday dates and compensatory workdays. If the next annual schedule has not been issued, the guide must say **not yet published** rather than projecting dates or inventing a crowd index.

Then list every hard commitment: international flights, named trains, timed attraction entries, performances, hotel cancellation deadlines and any closure day that changes the route. A fixed booking needs a buffer proportionate to what happens if it is missed. If it sits after an arrival or intercity move, ask whether a flexible block would protect the trip better.

For attraction purchases, [official or reseller tickets](/guides/official-or-reseller-china-tickets/) explains how to identify the ticket owner, pass identity details correctly, distinguish a request from a confirmed booking, and compare cancellation and refund terms. The venue’s current official notice remains the final operational source.

## 8. Let the least flexible traveller set the pace

Group pace is not an average. Children, older parents, wheelchair users, travellers managing fatigue, and people with substantial luggage may need different transfer margins or recovery blocks. Use the dedicated owner rather than forcing everyone into a generic template: [China with young children](/guides/china-itinerary-with-young-children/), [China with older parents](/guides/china-itinerary-with-older-parents/), or [wheelchair-accessible route planning](/guides/wheelchair-accessible-china-route-planning/).

Protect three kinds of margin:

1. **operational margin** before departures, timed entry and last admission;
2. **recovery margin** after long travel, late arrival or a demanding walking day;
3. **choice margin** that allows one optional stop to be dropped without breaking the booked chain.

The final night deserves its own check. [Where to spend the last night before an international flight](/guides/china-last-night-before-international-flight/) weighs airport access, morning risk and the value of one more city night.

## 9. Validate what can change, then choose the next step

One week before departure—and again after any major schedule change—verify entry documents, exact terminals and stations, booked names and passport numbers, attraction status, hotel confirmation, holiday effects and the recovery plan for the most important booking. Use official sources for current operational facts; use Homeground guides to understand which fact changes the decision.

If the route survives that check, keep planning independently. If you have a usable day-by-day route but want a human stress test, compare **Route Review**. If you have dates and priorities but no workable route, compare **Route Build**. If bookings or local coordination must be included, discuss **Full Trip Planning & Ground Support**. The service owner is [China Itinerary Review & Route Planning](/china-itinerary-review/).

You can also send a shared brief with dates or total nights, party size, candidate places, pace or mobility needs and a rough budget. A Homeground planner reads it, confirms the suitable scope, price or quote and delivery timing, and sends payment instructions separately if you proceed. The first enquiry is not a free itinerary, an automatic result or the start of paid work.
