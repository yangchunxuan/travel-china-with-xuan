# China itinerary planning: build a first trip that fits the nights you have

There is no single best first-trip itinerary for China. The useful route is the one that fits your **hotel nights, arrival and departure windows, entry and exit gateways, priority experiences and real travel pace**. Start by protecting complete sightseeing days, then add cities only when each one contributes something distinct. A fast train does not make the hotel checkout, station journey, boarding, arrival and next check-in disappear.

Use this Hub to turn a wish list into a route skeleton. It will not give you an hour-by-hour private itinerary or pretend that a generic 7-, 10- or 14-day template fits every flight and traveller. When a choice becomes specific, follow the narrower Homeground owner for entry, transport, accommodation, holidays, tickets or a named city sequence.

## Start with six decisions, not a list of famous cities

Write down six things before deciding the order:

1. **Nights:** the number of hotel nights inside China, not a rounded marketing label such as “two weeks.”
2. **Gateways:** the airport or border where the trip enters and the one where it leaves. Keep them provisional until flight times and fares are compared.
3. **Priorities:** three or four experiences that would make the trip feel worthwhile. These may be places, landscapes, food, museums or time with people—not ten equally urgent pins.
4. **City roles:** what each proposed overnight base contributes that another one does not.
5. **Fixed constraints:** international flights, identity-linked tickets, closures, public holidays, events and any appointment that cannot move easily.
6. **Pace:** how the actual group handles early starts, long walks, luggage, unfamiliar stations and consecutive transfer days.

This order prevents a common trap: choosing five city names, buying one flight, and only then discovering that the route has too few usable days or returns to the original gateway for no good reason.

If entry eligibility is unresolved, stop the route work at a provisional level and use the [China Essentials Hub](/essentials/) and the official source relevant to the traveller’s passport, purpose and route. A planning page cannot determine personal immigration eligibility.

## Turn hotel nights into usable sightseeing days

Hotel nights are a clean starting unit because they expose arrival, departure and relocation days. They do not, however, equal complete days.

Make a simple ledger with one row for every calendar date:

| Day type | What qualifies | How to treat it before bookings |
|---|---|---|
| Complete sightseeing day | The traveller wakes and sleeps in the same base, with no major arrival or departure chain | Protect it for the experiences that need time and energy |
| Fragment day | Arrival, departure or a manageable transfer leaves a useful but limited block | Give it a flexible, replaceable activity near the route |
| Transfer-dominant day | Checkout, terminal access, waiting, intercity travel, arrival and check-in consume the useful middle | Do not count it as a second full sightseeing day |
| Protected buffer | Time deliberately kept free before a high-consequence flight, fixed booking or difficult connection | Spend it only after the risk has passed |

A late-night arrival may create a hotel night without creating a sightseeing day. A morning departure can remove most of the final calendar date. A midday train may leave a morning and an evening, yet divide attention so thoroughly that neither block suits a large museum or distant landscape.

When flight times are not known, keep a range: “four complete days, possibly five” is more honest than choosing the optimistic endpoint. The public guide [Is your China itinerary too rushed?](/guides/is-your-china-itinerary-too-rushed/) explains the full pace diagnosis. This Hub does not run an automated route calculator or turn unknown assumptions into a score.

## Give every overnight base a distinct job

A first trip becomes easier to edit when cities are treated as roles instead of trophies.

| Possible role | The question it answers | Evidence that the base belongs |
|---|---|---|
| International gateway | Where can the trip enter or leave with workable flight times? | The complete airport-to-hotel or hotel-to-airport chain supports the route |
| Cultural anchor | Which place deserves multiple complete days for the traveller’s strongest interest? | Several priority experiences cluster without daily cross-city travel |
| Landscape contrast | Which place changes the trip’s physical setting? | It offers a distinct reason to stay, not just another famous name |
| Regional connector | Which base makes nearby tasks easier? | The day trips or onward legs are genuinely coherent from that base |
| Recovery base | Where can the group slow down, do laundry or absorb disruption? | It reduces pressure rather than adding another complex move |

Two cities with the same job compete for limited nights. Ask which one serves the stated priorities better, then remove or defer the other. A short first trip often improves when one weak base is cut and its nights are returned to the anchors.

Do not infer a universal maximum number of cities from the trip length alone. A group using adjacent rail-linked bases at a steady pace faces a different route from a group mixing airports, mountain gateways and fixed-entry attractions. Browse [trip length and city order](/plan/trip-length-city-order/) for approved named decisions; do not create a new page for every city-and-day permutation.

## Count every transfer from hotel door to hotel door

Scheduled train or flight time is only the middle of a relocation. Before choosing a connection, write down:

- checkout and luggage handling;
- travel from the hotel to the correct station or airport terminal;
- security, document checks, wayfinding and a sensible pre-departure margin;
- the scheduled journey and realistic disruption exposure;
- movement from the arrival platform or baggage hall to the next hotel;
- check-in, food and the energy needed to restart sightseeing.

Treat this as **transfer burden**, not as a promise that every move costs the same number of hours. A centrally located high-speed-rail trip can preserve more of a day than a short flight whose airports sit far from both hotels. An overnight train can save a hotel night but still reduce sleep and next-day capacity. Read [night train or daytime high-speed rail](/guides/china-night-train-or-daytime-high-speed-rail/) for that decision and the [first-time high-speed train guide](/guides/china-high-speed-train-first-time-guide/) for execution.

Hotel changes also matter inside one city. Packing, storing bags, reaching a second property and waiting for a room can consume the same flexible hours a traveller hoped to protect. Use the [Where to Stay Hub](/stay/) to choose a base by the arrival day, hardest sightseeing day and departure day—not by a station icon alone.

## Choose the route geometry before the exact sequence

Most first-trip routes use one of three shapes:

| Shape | When it helps | Failure mode to test |
|---|---|---|
| Open-jaw | The route naturally advances across China and flights can enter one gateway and leave another | A higher fare or poor flight timing may erase the saved backtracking |
| Round trip to one gateway | Air access is much stronger from one city or the trip stays within one region | The final return can consume a day and put a separate ticket before the international flight |
| Hub-and-spoke or hybrid | Several visits cluster around a stable base, with only one or two advancing moves | Repeated day trips may become longer than one hotel relocation |

Use [open-jaw flights and route planning](/guides/china-open-jaw-flights-route-planning/) to compare complete gateway chains. Use [hub-and-spoke or multiple bases](/guides/china-hub-and-spoke-or-multi-base-route/) when the unresolved issue is whether to keep one hotel. Do not choose a geometry because it looks tidy on a map; compare the door-to-door movements it creates.

The last international departure deserves its own protection. If the final domestic leg and long-haul ticket are separate, ask what happens when the first one is delayed or cancelled. [Where to spend the last night before an international flight](/guides/china-last-night-before-international-flight/) owns that decision.

## Place fixed bookings only after the route can survive them

Fixed reservations form a constraint network. An attraction ticket may depend on the correct passport name, an arrival time may depend on immigration and baggage, and an intercity departure may depend on leaving a large site early. One tight assumption can make three bookings fragile.

Place constraints in this order:

1. international arrival and departure;
2. immovable personal or event dates;
3. scarce, identity-linked or timed priorities;
4. intercity relocation days;
5. flexible museums, neighbourhoods, meals and rest blocks.

An arrival day should usually carry an activity that can be shortened, moved or dropped. Only place a scarce timed anchor there when the complete arrival chain has enough margin and the loss is acceptable. The [arrival-day anchor or flexible block guide](/guides/china-arrival-day-booked-anchor-or-flexible-block/) provides the detailed risk tree.

Check the [national public-holiday travel calendar](/guides/china-public-holidays-travel-calendar/) before treating any week as ordinary. Official holiday dates, adjusted working days and venue operations can change the transport, hotel and ticket problem. Then use [official or reseller tickets](/guides/official-or-reseller-china-tickets/) to decide which booking channel and evidence standard fit a particular attraction. Neither Hub promises live availability.

## Match the route to the people taking it

“Fast” and “slow” are not personality labels. Translate pace into observable route choices: consecutive early starts, walking exposure, luggage handling, sleep after night transport, meal flexibility and how much recovery follows a difficult day.

Consider three groups using the same list of places:

- **A light-packing couple with open-jaw flights** may accept several rail-linked bases, provided each move leads forward and fixed tickets remain separated from transfer risk.
- **A family with young children** may keep the same anchors but use fewer hotel changes, later starts and flexible evening blocks. The [China itinerary with young children](/guides/china-itinerary-with-young-children/) owns the child-specific chain.
- **Travellers using a wheelchair or needing step-free continuity** must verify the whole access chain: vehicle, station, lift, toilet, hotel room and venue fallback. A single ramp is not proof. Use the [wheelchair-accessible route-planning guide](/guides/wheelchair-accessible-china-route-planning/).

Older travellers are not automatically slow, and younger travellers are not automatically resilient. Ask what happens after two difficult mornings or an interrupted night. The traveller-fit collection keeps these constraints in their own evidenced owners rather than generating a template for every age and duration.

## Validate the skeleton in the right order

Do not book every layer at once. Validate from high consequence to replaceable:

1. **Entry:** verify the official rule for the passport, purpose, permitted stay and route.
2. **Gateways:** compare the international flights and the complete ground transfer at both ends.
3. **Route legs:** confirm that each city pair has a plausible transport mode and that the exact station or airport will be checked before purchase.
4. **Hotel bases:** verify location, foreign-guest handling, room/access needs and cancellation terms at property level.
5. **Fixed priorities:** check official ticket, closure, identity and holiday rules for the actual date.
6. **Budget:** include intercity travel, local transfers, admissions, hotel changes and recovery choices—not only headline fares. Use [How much does a China trip cost?](/guides/how-much-does-a-china-trip-cost/) for the full budget frame.
7. **Recovery:** decide what can move, what can be dropped and which final-day connection must be protected.

Use the [Getting Around Hub](/transport/) for station, airport and city-pair execution. Recheck all dynamic facts before payment: schedules, inventory, fares, opening hours, ticket-release rules, holiday arrangements and hotel policies can change.

## Repair an overloaded route by removing dependencies

When the skeleton fails, do not merely shorten every visit. Remove the dependency creating the largest consequence.

- If two weak cities require two hotel moves, cut one base and return the nights to the strongest anchor.
- If the route doubles back, compare an open-jaw gateway or reverse the direction before removing sightseeing time.
- If a fixed attraction sits on an arrival day, move it to a complete day and give the arrival a replaceable neighbourhood, meal or rest block.
- If a domestic connection threatens the international departure, move to the departure gateway earlier or add a protected final night.
- If an event, venue or transport option disappears, preserve the city role with a different activity rather than forcing another long transfer.

A good recovery plan names the first thing to drop. “Everything is essential” is not a route; it is a list with no failure policy.
