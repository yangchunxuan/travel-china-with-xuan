## Central decision requested

Keep A8 as an internal collection model, not an ordinary national nightlife article. The reader need is real, but the useful unit is a verified place-and-evening task: where public life gathers after dark, what kind of space it is, how the visitor reaches and leaves it, and what changes by day, weather, season or local management. A national page that strings together famous markets would conceal those differences and would compete with destination and route owners.

The collection should become eligible for public review only after at least three independently useful, trilingual, indexable owner pages exist and represent at least two different spatial forms. Meeting that count would establish eligibility for review, not automatic indexation.

## User task and information gain

The collection should help a traveller answer:

> Which verified evening place fits the city and date I am already visiting, what kind of public or commercial space is it, and how do I complete the evening without assuming that every Chinese city has the same “night market” experience?

Its information gain is the distinction among spatial forms and operating conditions. A night market, a lit commercial street, a public waterfront, a museum district with extended hours and a neighbourhood food street are not synonyms. The collection navigation should expose that difference before it exposes city names.

## Proposed collection model

| Internal facet | Reader question | Qualifying owner shape | Does not qualify |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `evening-market-food-street` | Is this a current market, a managed food street or only a promotional event? | One verified place with current operating source, arrival/exit plan and closure fallback | A “top night markets” list or copied platform hours |
| `evening-waterfront-public-space` | Is the main value walking, views, culture or paid consumption? | A bounded public-space route with access edges, lighting limits and last-mile recovery | A skyline gallery or undated lighting promise |
| `evening-heritage-culture-district` | What remains open after daytime attractions close? | A district sequence separating public space, venue hours and ticketed programming | A museum-opening-hours fragment |
| `neighbourhood-evening-life` | Where does ordinary local evening activity gather without turning residents into an attraction? | A public-route task with privacy, noise and residential-boundary guidance | “Authentic locals” voyeurism or intrusion into residential lanes |
| `event-or-seasonal-evening` | Is the experience permanent, recurring or a short pilot? | A maintained owner that states the date window and stable fallback | A permanent canonical for one temporary market |

Discovery may additionally filter by city entity, but city is not the primary collection logic and does not create city-by-month pages.

## Admission rules for a future child page

A page belongs only when all of these are true:

1. It names one bounded place, route or comparison and one traveller outcome.
2. A current government, venue, operator or district source verifies identity and operating/access facts.
3. Temporary programming is dated and has a stable fallback; it is not written as permanent inventory.
4. Public, commercial and residential boundaries are distinguished.
5. The return chain is practical: last public transport, legal pickup point or a clearly labelled live check.
6. The copy states what changes the recommendation, including weekday, season, weather, event dates, construction or crowd controls where relevant.
7. English, Chinese and Korean versions solve the same full task.
8. Real documentary imagery has exact place, capture date or known limitation, creator and reuse rights.

## Hard exclusions

- No national “best nightlife cities” or “top 10 night markets” article.
- No city × month × audience combinations.
- No copied opening hours, vendor counts, event calendars or “24-hour city” slogans without a retrieval date and current owner.
- No claim that night markets are a universal or timeless form of Chinese urban life.
- No treating government designation as proof of present quality, crowd size, opening on a particular night or visitor fit.
- No residential voyeurism, drone instruction, restricted-space entry or identifiable close-up of people without permission.
- No AI-generated street, market, waterfront, crowd or documentary nightlife image.

## Source-backed rationale

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism’s third national list shows that a recognised night-time culture and tourism cluster may be a street, cultural venue cluster, commercial complex, scenic area or other managed zone; its notice also calls for service quality, safety and order. The first-batch construction notice similarly describes several eligible physical forms. These are policy categories, not evidence that every listed area is open or suitable on a traveller’s date.

Shanghai provides one concrete planning example. Its official Huangpu River/Suzhou Creek plan connects continuous waterfront public space, cultural uses, commerce and night-time activity. A later municipal response describes a network of waterfront belts, commercial areas and markets. These sources support spatial variety; they do not establish a national formula.

Independent research reinforces the need for caution. Research on Pearl River Delta urban-village public spaces found several kinds of night-time public space rather than one market form. Current night-market and waterfront-vitality studies also examine how convenience, comfort, sensory conditions, demographics and management shape use. Those findings justify fields for public-space form and user conditions, but case-study results must not be universalised to all Chinese cities.

## Dynamic facts and refresh policy

- Collection architecture: review every 12 months.
- Child operating details: review immediately before publication and on operator/district notice change.
- Seasonal markets and pilots: review for the exact event window; expire or fold the record after the event.
- Transit, waterfront closures, construction, crowd controls and lighting programmes: treat as live conditions, never evergreen guarantees.
- National or local “night economy” designations: record issuing authority and date; do not infer present opening.
