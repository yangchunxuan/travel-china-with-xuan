## 1. Publication controls

### Risk and review cadence

| Risk | Meaning | Maximum age at publication | Routine review |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **High** | A stale claim can cause denied boarding/entry, an immigration or customs breach, loss of money, or a first-day dead end. | Verify again on publication day; underlying research no more than 7 days old. | Weekly, and again when an official trigger occurs. |
| **Medium** | A stale claim can waste material time/money or break an important setup path, but there is a safe offline fallback. | Verify within 30 days and on publication day if a product/channel is named. | Monthly. |
| **Low** | Durable legal or service-owner fact with a stable official fallback. | Verify within 90 days; confirm the source still resolves before publication. | Quarterly or annually as specified. |

### Global invalidation rule

1. Any official expiry, superseding announcement, changed country/port/province/model list, redirected government host, or mismatch between two competent authorities turns the row **red**.
2. A red row must not remain as an unqualified traveler-facing answer. Remove the exact number/list/availability claim, replace it with a decision prompt to the competent authority, and route the user to the row's fallback.
3. Do not use an airline, reseller, forum, search snippet or AI summary to overrule the competent Chinese authority. An airline may still impose a boarding-document check; that is a separate operational check.
4. Preserve the official URL, visible revision/publication date, access timestamp and a screenshot/PDF copy in the release evidence pack. If the official page is JavaScript-only, preserve the landing URL and a human-verified screen capture.
5. The hub should link to canonical detail owners. It must not duplicate volatile country lists, port lists, payment fees, carrier packages or device model matrices.

## 2. Current boundary snapshot for editors

These are the findings that affect canonical boundaries on this research date. They still require the row-level pre-publication checks below.

- The current MFA unilateral-waiver FAQ names **50 countries**. It includes Canada and the UK; their ordinary-passport waiver began 2026-02-17 and runs through 2026-12-31. It does **not** name the United States. The US owner therefore must not imply that US ordinary-passport holders receive the 30-day unilateral waiver.
- Singapore ordinary-passport holders are covered by the China–Singapore mutual visa-exemption agreement, effective 2024-02-09, for stays no longer than 30 days within the agreement's permitted purposes. This is a different owner/rule from unilateral visa exemption.
- The NIA 240-hour transit route currently covers **55 nationalities, 65 entry ports and permitted areas in 24 provincial-level regions**. UK, Canada, US and Singapore are all on the nationality list. It requires, among other things, an international travel document valid for at least three months and confirmed onward travel to a third country/region within 240 hours; it is not a substitute label for an ordinary China visit.
- The NIA online arrival-card service has operated since 2025-11-20 and the official web form is free. Separately, Homeground's `china-online-arrival-card` owner is now published/indexable in EN/ZH/KO and present once per locale in the live sitemap, based on production readback on 2026-08-21. That live detail owner does **not** publish or authorize the separate First 24 Hours Hub.
- Non-hotel accommodation registration remains due within 24 hours. As of 2026-08-20, NIA's national online process is still a pilot in **Hebei, Liaoning, Zhejiang, Hubei, Guangxi, Chongqing and Sichuan**. Do not present it as nationwide. In-person registration remains available.
- Mainland mobile eSIM support changed materially in 2026 and is model, market and operator specific. The 2026 multi-agency guide only says some operators support eSIM and tells users to check current requirements. Do not reuse the old blanket claim that mainland phone eSIM is unavailable; equally, do not claim that every foreign phone or passport can activate a mainland eSIM.
- Payment-app card brands, limits, fees, identity checks and issuer authorization remain product-specific. The current multi-agency guide expressly sends readers to the live product page and service agreement.
