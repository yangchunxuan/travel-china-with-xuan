# Shanghai travel guide for a first trip: days, areas, airports and the next city

Shanghai is three decisions at once: an international gateway, a city worth visiting in its own right, and the strongest transport node for a wider Yangtze Delta route. A useful first-trip plan therefore does more than list the Bund, Yu Garden and a skyline observatory. It decides how many **complete sightseeing days** Shanghai receives, which side of the Huangpu River anchors the stay, whether Pudong or Hongqiao controls the arrival, and whether Suzhou, Hangzhou or Nanjing is a day visit or the next hotel.

For most first-time visitors, **three complete city days plus the arrival and departure nights is the most balanced stay**. Two complete days cover the essential historical-and-modern contrast with careful selection. Four create room for a museum, neighbourhood time or a weather buffer. Shanghai Disneyland is separate: count it as another full day and consider an extra night when children, an early park start or the next departure justify one.

This hub owns those broad choices. The linked airport, accommodation, Disneyland and regional-transport guides own the changing execution details.

## Is Shanghai the right stop for your trip?

Shanghai suits travellers who want to understand contemporary urban China while retaining historical texture. It is not an old capital in the Beijing or Xi’an sense. Its strength is the relationship between a trading waterfront, treaty-port-era architecture, Chinese garden and lane-house traditions, museums, dense commercial districts, design culture, neighbourhood life and a metropolitan transport system.

| Traveller | Shanghai fit | What makes it work | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| First visit to China | Strong, especially at entry or exit | International access, clear urban contrasts and onward rail | Counting arrival day as a city day |
| Urban, architecture or design traveller | Very strong | Bund–Pudong views, reuse projects, streets and museums | Seeing only towers and shopping streets |
| Family with children | Strong when days have one purpose | Parks, river views, museums and Disneyland can be separated | Compressing long-haul arrival, city touring and Disney |
| Short stopover traveller | Good for one compact city slice | Central hotel and one coherent Puxi cluster | Repeatedly crossing the metropolis |
| Traveller prioritising imperial sites or landscapes | Better within a wider China route | A modern counterpoint to Beijing, Xi’an or Zhangjiajie | Expecting Shanghai to replace them |
| Traveller using Shanghai only as an airport | Often a missed opportunity | Two complete days can reveal a coherent city | Keeping every night at a remote airport hotel |

Shanghai can sit at either end of China. At the beginning it offers international access and a comparatively legible urban landing. At the end it provides flight choice, a flexible final base and a buffer before departure. Flight geography and onward direction should decide.

## Read Shanghai from west to east, not as a list of attractions

The Huangpu River is the simplest organising line. Most first-trip history and neighbourhood time sits on the **west bank in Puxi**. From the Bund, the classic view looks east to **Lujiazui in Pudong**: early commercial waterfront architecture faces a late-twentieth- and twenty-first-century skyline. That two-bank relationship is the city’s central visual argument.

Within Puxi, three layers matter. The Bund, Old City and Yu Garden form a waterfront-and-historic cluster. People’s Square and East Nanjing Road form a civic, museum and commercial spine. Jing’an and the broad travel area called the Former French Concession offer a smaller scale of tree-lined streets, lane houses, cafés, shops and longer walks. Their edges overlap, but their travel jobs differ.

Hongqiao is much farther west. It is an airport, railway and exhibition hub, not a central sightseeing district. Disneyland and the International Resort area are far east and likewise require their own operational plan.

## How many days do you need in Shanghai?

Count only days that begin and end in Shanghai without an intercity move. A mid-afternoon international arrival is an arrival day; a morning checkout followed by a high-speed train is a transfer day. This prevents a nominal four-day stay from becoming only two usable days.

| Complete sightseeing time | What it adds | Realistic structure | Leave out |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 complete days | Essential Puxi history and Pudong contrast | Day 1: Bund–Old City–Yu Garden; Day 2: People’s Square plus Jing’an/Former French Concession or Lujiazui | A major museum plus several neighbourhoods, a regional trip and Disney |
| 3 complete days | One deeper layer | Add a museum block, slower neighbourhood day or dedicated river/Pudong day | Do not also force Suzhou, Hangzhou and Disney into the stay |
| 4 complete days | Buffer and one focused interest | Keep three city days, then add architecture, food, art or an easier day | Do not scatter across every suburb |
| Disneyland | A separate full day | Plan transport, park strategy, children’s energy and the following morning together | Do not count it inside the 2/3/4 city-day comparison |
| Suzhou, Hangzhou or Nanjing | A regional-route choice | Make one focused day visit or move to the next city | Do not call repeated rail excursions “seeing Shanghai” |

With two days, choose contrast over coverage. Keep one day mainly west of the river; use the second for civic Shanghai, neighbourhood Shanghai or Pudong according to interest. A third day is most valuable when it slows one layer down: an unhurried museum, a neighbourhood without hourly reservations, or a river-and-Pudong day. A fourth improves quality by absorbing weather, closure days, late arrival or fatigue rather than merely adding distant sights.

### Travel scenario 1: a first China trip with three complete Shanghai days

A couple arrives at PVG in the afternoon, stays near People’s Square and leaves from Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station four mornings later. The arrival evening remains local. Day one links the Old City, Yu Garden and the Bund. Day two places a museum or People’s Square before East Nanjing Road and the river. Day three uses Jing’an and selected Former French Concession streets, adding Pudong only if a skyline experience is a genuine priority.

This produces three different readings of Shanghai while protecting the long-haul arrival. It does not count landing day, a Suzhou excursion or the train morning as another city day.

## Where to stay: give the hotel one job

The best area reduces the trip’s most expensive repeated movement. A first visitor should normally stay in central Puxi unless an early flight, railway-heavy plan, exhibition or Disneyland gives another location a clear advantage.

| Area | Best job | Strengths | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bund / East Nanjing Road | Keep the iconic waterfront close | Immediate atmosphere and easy Bund access | Crowds; some hotels are less metro-convenient than the label suggests |
| People’s Square | Make a mixed first visit simple | Interchange, museums, East Nanjing Road and balanced Puxi reach | Busy and less intimate than residential streets |
| Jing’an / Former French Concession | Prioritise streets, dining and evening choice | Tree-lined walks, lane-house areas, cafés and central connections | The travel label is broad; the exact metro stop matters |
| Lujiazui | Prioritise Pudong business or skyline hotels | Towers, river views and business infrastructure | Many first-trip sights remain across the river |
| Hongqiao | Protect a flight, high-speed train or exhibition | Close to SHA and Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station | Weak default for walking the main visitor districts |

The Bund or East Nanjing Road gives a recognisable first evening. People’s Square is the safest all-round base for a mixed plan. Jing’an or a well-connected part of the Former French Concession suits travellers who value streets, food and evenings more than waking beside a headline sight. Lujiazui is rational for a specific Pudong task; “Pudong” alone is too large to guarantee convenience. Hongqiao solves transport rather than the city experience.

Use the detailed [Shanghai first-trip accommodation guide](/guides/shanghai-where-to-stay-first-trip/). Before booking, identify the nearest metro entrance and walking distance, not only the district name.

## PVG, SHA and the Airport Link: which arrival changes the plan?

Shanghai Pudong International Airport (PVG) and Shanghai Hongqiao International Airport (SHA) are separate airports. PVG, east of the centre, is the main long-haul international gateway. SHA is west and can be materially easier for the Hongqiao rail hub or a western Shanghai address. Always read the airport code and terminal on the ticket.

| Airport or link | Best use | City relationship | Planning caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PVG | Most long-haul international arrivals and departures | Metro Line 2, Maglev via Longyang Road, Airport Link and road transport | Immigration, baggage and the final city transfer still take time |
| SHA Terminal 1 | Flights using Hongqiao’s older terminal | Metro Line 10 | Do not apply the T2–rail-hub relationship to T1 |
| SHA Terminal 2 | Flights benefiting from the Hongqiao hub | Metro Lines 2/10 and Airport Link; within the wider hub | Airport and railway stations remain distinct facilities |
| Airport Link | Airport-to-airport and selected suburban connections | Links Hongqiao Airport T2 and PVG, including International Resort en route | Not a direct Bund train; the last hotel transfer remains |
| Metro Line 2 | The main east–west urban spine | Connects PVG, Lujiazui, People’s Square and the Hongqiao hub area | Cheap but tiring end to end with luggage |
| Maglev | Fast PVG–Longyang Road segment | Requires another transfer at Longyang Road | Not a one-seat airport-to-Bund service |

The Airport Link most improves **PVG–Hongqiao transfers** and some resort-area journeys. It does not make PVG central, and Hongqiao Airport Terminal 2 Station is not Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station. Allow walking, wayfinding, security and a margin for the next departure. Operating hours and intervals are dynamic.

For a central hotel, Metro Line 2 is easy to understand; a taxi or verified pickup may be easier after a long flight, with children or with heavy luggage. The Maglev is useful when the Longyang Road transfer fits the hotel route, not automatically because it is fast.

Treat a late-night arrival as logistics, not sightseeing. Check what still operates after immigration and baggage, then use the official taxi rank, a verified pickup or current airport night transport. Compare one late transfer into the centre with the cost of changing hotels next morning. The detailed choice belongs to [Pudong or Hongqiao airport for Shanghai](/guides/shanghai-pudong-or-hongqiao-airport/).

## Shanghai railway stations: the city name is not enough

A search result labelled “Shanghai” may use a station on the opposite side of the metropolis from the hotel. Search the exact station in China Railway 12306 and calculate hotel-to-station time before paying.

| Station | Practical role | Hotel relationship | Regional relevance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station | Main high-speed hub for many national and delta services | Excellent for Hongqiao; a deliberate cross-city transfer from central Puxi or Pudong | Often useful for Suzhou, Hangzhou and Nanjing, subject to the train |
| Shanghai Railway Station | Central/northern Puxi station with a different service mix | Often easier from Jing’an, People’s Square and northern central areas | Some nearby and long-distance services use it instead of Hongqiao |
| Shanghai South Railway Station | Southern Puxi conventional, sleeper and selected regional services | Can suit Xuhui or southern addresses | Recheck the current departure station in 12306 |
| Shanghai Songjiang Railway Station | Southwestern station serving Songjiang and selected corridors | Useful only when hotel or route aligns with the southwest | Not a central-station substitute merely because its name includes Shanghai |

Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station and SHA Terminal 2 share the wider Hongqiao hub, but the transfer still requires signs and time. Do not assume every high-speed train uses Hongqiao. South and Songjiang matter only when the exact train and geography support them. Future stations must not enter a live itinerary before official opening and bookable 12306 service.

For Hangzhou, use the [Shanghai–Hangzhou station and route guide](/guides/shanghai-hangzhou-transport-route/). For the wider region, use the [Shanghai–Suzhou–Hangzhou–Nanjing route-order guide](/guides/shanghai-suzhou-hangzhou-nanjing-route-order/). The exact station pair matters more than a headline journey time.

## Five spatial tasks, not five “top attractions”

### 1. Bund–Old City–Yu Garden: river and older urban core

Yu Garden, the City God Temple surroundings and the Old City lead naturally toward the Bund. The scale shifts from enclosed garden, market and lanes to the former financial waterfront; Lujiazui completes the view across the Huangpu. The point is to see how Chinese urban traditions, international commercial history and the modern skyline meet within one geography. Separate the ticketed garden from the surrounding commercial area and recheck current entry rules.

### 2. People’s Square–East Nanjing Road: civic and commercial axis

People’s Square is a transport and cultural hinge that can anchor a museum visit before East Nanjing Road and the Bund. Shanghai Museum’s People’s Square and East Museum sites have different locations, closure days and exhibitions. Decide which venue matters before writing “Shanghai Museum” into the day; a museum block must replace something rather than disappear between shopping and a night view.

### 3. Jing’an–Former French Concession: neighbourhood-scale Shanghai

Jing’an combines major commercial streets, a temple area, offices and strong transport. The broad Former French Concession travel area spans parts of present-day districts and contains lane houses, former residences, smaller institutions, shops and cafés. It is not a single gated quarter. Choose connected streets or one theme instead of zigzagging between scattered social-media locations.

### 4. Lujiazui: read the skyline from both banks

The west bank gives the classic skyline view; the east bank places the traveller among the towers and looks back to the historical waterfront. One observatory is usually enough, and weather should decide whether a paid high view is worthwhile. Pudong extends far beyond Lujiazui, so neither name should be treated as one compact sightseeing zone.

### 5. Disneyland and the outer city: a separate mission

Shanghai Disneyland is an eastern-resort task involving tickets, opening strategy, transport, children’s stamina and the next morning. The Airport Link’s Shanghai International Resort Station may help some airport journeys, but it is not the park gate. Use the [PVG-to-Shanghai-Disneyland guide](/guides/pudong-airport-to-shanghai-disneyland/) before planning luggage around it.

Water towns, coastal areas, Songjiang, Chongming and Yangshan also consume real travel time. Yangshan Phase IV is a controlled working port, not a walk-in attraction; the [Yangshan automated-port explainer](/guides/yangshan-automated-port-explained/) owns that access boundary. Check any claim of all-night public access against the [Shanghai 24-hour parks reality check](/guides/shanghai-24-hour-parks-reality-check/).

### Travel scenario 2: a family combining Shanghai and Disneyland

A family lands at PVG in the evening, wants two central days and one Disney day, then travels to Hangzhou. The weak version cycles through airport, Disney and central hotels before crossing to Hongqiao. The stronger version compares one central base with one purposeful Disney night. A resort night may earn the move for young children, an early park start and late exit; staying central may be simpler when Disney sits between central days and transport is manageable.

Disney remains a separate full day. The following morning should not rely on a late park exit and a tight train from the other side of the city. Decide with the exact hotel, park date, children’s ages, luggage and departure station.

## Suzhou, Hangzhou or Nanjing: day trip or next city?

The train ride is only the middle of the day. Add the hotel-to-station journey, security, waiting, arrival transfer and the forced return to Shanghai.

| City | Day-trip case | Next-city case | First-route default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Suzhou | One focused garden, canal or museum area and a suitable station | Early/late local time matters, interests are dispersed or onward travel removes the return | Strongest day-trip candidate, not automatic |
| Hangzhou | One tightly defined central priority | West Lake, tea areas, Lingyin or morning/evening atmosphere matter | Usually better as the next base |
| Nanjing | Very early start and one narrow purpose | Museums, city-wall history and dispersed sites matter | Usually a next city, not a default day trip |

Suzhou’s compact first-visit priorities can justify a day visit. Hangzhou often rewards a local night because its major experiences do not sit beside one station. Nanjing normally deserves its own base if it matters enough to include. A two-base Yangtze Delta plan can outperform both repeated returns and constant hotel changes.

Do not rewrite those cities here. Use the regional route-order guide, then verify exact trains in 12306.

## Should Shanghai come first or last in a China itinerary?

Put Shanghai first when the international arrival works, the group wants an accessible urban landing and the route continues without backtracking. Put it last when the departure is stronger or a flexible buffer before flying home is valuable. An open-jaw route—arriving in one Chinese city and leaving from another—often removes a needless return.

From Beijing or Zhangjiajie, compare the actual airport or station and hotel-arrival time, not only the ticket duration. The [Beijing–Zhangjiajie–Shanghai transport guide](/guides/beijing-zhangjiajie-shanghai-transport/) shows that door-to-door calculation.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many nights do I need in Shanghai?

Four nights commonly create three complete sightseeing days, provided arrival and departure do not consume them. Three nights can support two complete days. Protect another night or the following morning when Disneyland, a late arrival or an early train changes the structure.

### Should I fly into PVG or SHA?

Choose by international availability and the total route, then compare ground transport. PVG is the main long-haul gateway. SHA can be easier for western Shanghai and Hongqiao rail connections. Use the exact airport, terminal, hotel and next departure.

### Should I stay near the Bund or in the Former French Concession?

Choose the Bund or East Nanjing Road for immediate waterfront atmosphere. Choose Jing’an or a connected part of the Former French Concession for neighbourhood walking, restaurants and a less monumental rhythm. Check the exact metro entrance because both labels cover varied locations.

### Is Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station better than Shanghai Railway Station?

Neither is universally better. Hongqiao handles many high-speed services and connects with SHA Terminal 2 but lies far west. Shanghai Railway Station can be easier from central and northern Puxi and may have the required train. Book by the actual service and hotel transfer.

### How does the Airport Link change the airport choice?

It speeds travel between PVG and Hongqiao Airport Terminal 2 and serves selected intermediate stations. It does not go directly to the Bund, and the airport station is not Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station. Allow for the last transfer and recheck the timetable.

### Do I need to change hotels for Shanghai Disneyland?

Not automatically. A resort hotel earns the move when an early start, late exit, young children or a nearby airport outweighs packing. A central hotel can be simpler when Disney is one isolated day between city days. Use the exact park date and next departure.

### Is Suzhou or Hangzhou worth a day trip from Shanghai?

Suzhou is often the stronger candidate when the visit is focused and station geography works. Hangzhou is possible, but West Lake, Lingyin, tea areas and local morning or evening time often justify an overnight. Calculate the complete door-to-door day.

### Should Shanghai be at the beginning or end of a China trip?

Either can work. Start there for an international landing and urban introduction; finish there for flight choice and a buffer. Prefer the order that avoids backtracking and protects the international departure.

### Can I land at PVG and take a train to Suzhou or Hangzhou the same day?

Only when arrival time, immigration, baggage, airport transfer, exact railway station and ticket buffer all work conservatively. A separate train is not protected by the flight. After a late or long-haul arrival, a Shanghai night is usually safer.

### Do I need to stay in Pudong to experience Shanghai?

No. Many first visitors are better in central Puxi and cross the river for Lujiazui. Stay in Pudong when a specific office, museum, skyline hotel, airport or resort creates a clear advantage. Pudong is too large to be one convenience label.
