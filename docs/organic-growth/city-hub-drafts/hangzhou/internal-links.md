## Placement by Hub module

### Section 1 — Hangzhou's route role

Use the Shanghai–Hangzhou station-pair guide and the four-city route-order guide. These links explain why a fast train is not the same as a good day trip and why Hangzhou may be a second base rather than another one-night stop.

### Section 3 — day return, two nights or three nights

Use the pace guide once. Do not add multiple generic “how many days” links. The Hub itself owns the Hangzhou time decision.

### Section 4 — where to stay

Use the hotel-near-metro guide after warning that “near West Lake” and “near a station” are marketing labels until the exact pin and entrance are checked.

### Section 5 — airport and railway stations

Use the Shanghai–Hangzhou station-pair guide first and the national high-speed-train guide second. The first chooses the pair; the second executes boarding.

### Section 6 — place clusters

- White Snake article: a narrow handoff from Broken Bridge/Leifeng evidence only.
- Tea-landscape article: a narrow handoff from the Longjing cluster only.
- Grand Canal article: a narrow handoff from the northern canal cluster only.
- Liangzhu article: a narrow handoff from the dedicated suburban-day block only.

Do not stack all six links after every cluster.

### Section 7 — onward route

Use the four-city route-order guide for Shanghai/Suzhou/Nanjing architecture and the Huangshan-base guide when Huangshan is a genuine onward region. The Hub needs no separate Wuzhen guide until one is published and verified.

### Section 9 — current Homeground articles

A consolidated ten-link reading module is appropriate because the heading explicitly tells the reader that these are current supporting owners. Descriptions must state the handoff job and avoid repeating article titles alone.

### Section 10 — natural planning entry

Use exactly two commercial/action links:

1. itinerary review for a traveller who already has a route;
2. whole-trip route planning for a traveller who still needs the route built.

Do not turn earlier informational sections into sales-link repetition.
