## Revised direct answer

For a first literary visit, treat Pu Songling Memorial Museum and the former-residence fabric as one core compound, not two competing attractions. Read the protected and restored building first, then objects and labels that connect Pu Songling to his writings. Use *Liaozhai Zhiyi* and local-life scholarship to understand why the work is more than a “ghost book.” Treat Liaozhai liqu as a separate vernacular sung corpus whose texts, tune patterns and performance history require their own evidence. Only then add modern Liaozhai City as evidence of later adaptation and entertainment. Liuquan and the cemetery are optional commemorative modules, not the spine.

## Revised narrative spine

**Former residence and museum evidence → the prose collection and local life → Liaozhai liqu → modern Liaozhai City.**

The visitor first learns what a restored author site and a labelled object can prove. The story then moves from Pu's documented rural and lower-literati world into two different bodies of writing: the classical-Chinese tales and vernacular sung works. The final turn enters a modern themed attraction and asks how later tourism converts literature into gardens, lighting and performance. Liuquan and the cemetery appear only as optional context after the evidence-led core.

Key turns:

1. The museum grew from the former-residence site, but the house was restored after wartime destruction; “former residence” is not a claim that every visible surface survived from Pu's lifetime.
2. Local life can illuminate the writer's concerns without turning a fictional person into a historical resident or every story into a transcript of Zichuan events.
3. Liaozhai liqu is not incidental theme-park music. Text, tune, language and performed transmission are distinct evidence objects.
4. Liaozhai City is a specific modern attraction in Pujiazhuang, Zichuan District. Its current staging documents reception, not Qing-period daily life.

### 5. What is Liaozhai liqu, and how is it different from the prose tales?

- Do not expand a debate about an individual work or textual witness into a claim that the entire system is disputed.

### 7. What can Liuquan or the cemetery add?

- Keep both optional.
- The cultural-relics record confirms them as attached protected sites; it does not confirm current public access, route or photography.
- Treat the tea-and-story account as folklore/tourism interpretation unless a documentary source is supplied.
- The cemetery supports biography and commemoration, not the fictional tales.
