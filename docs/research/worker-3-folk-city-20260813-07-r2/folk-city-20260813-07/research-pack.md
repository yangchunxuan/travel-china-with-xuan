## Direct answer that could be supported after the hold is cleared

A non-believer does not need to decide whether Mazu belief is “true” in order to understand why the ancestral temple matters. A future article can separate a traditional life narrative from documented institution and building history, and can distinguish an ordinary public visit from pilgrimage and major ritual. It can also show that “ancestral temple” does not mean every visible hall is early fabric: the temple’s own account says the present west-axis complex was rebuilt from the 1980s to 1994 and the south-axis Song-style group was built from 1998 to 2002.

What cannot yet be published is prescriptive entry advice. Without management confirmation and a named local review, Homeground cannot tell a foreign visitor exactly where to stand, whether a ritual may be observed, how to cross a threshold, whether participation is optional in every open hall, or what may be photographed. The honest current decision is HOLD.

## Evidence narrative

### 1. The international record is about a living practice, not a verdict on belief

UNESCO inscribed “Mazu belief and customs” on the Representative List in 2009. Its cautious wording describes Mazu as *believed* to have lived in the tenth century and frames the element through oral traditions, ceremonies, temple fairs and smaller practices. The 2009 committee decision records community and temple-organization participation in nomination and safeguarding.

This can confirm an internationally documented living heritage element and community role. It cannot prove every biographical episode, define one universal ritual, or guarantee that a visitor can watch or record an activity.

### 2. “Ancestral temple” and “old building” are not synonyms

The official temple page places the complex at Gongxia village on northern Meizhou Island and lists west-axis and south-axis groups. It also provides the crucial intervention chronology: the visible west-axis complex was rebuilt beginning in the 1980s and completed in 1994; the south axis began in 1998 and was completed in 2002 as a Song-style complex.

This supports an evidence-led material reading. It does not establish the surviving-material percentage of each building, nor does “Song-style” mean Song-dynasty fabric. A future caption must identify a building, its axis and its documented construction/intervention status rather than calling the entire view “the thousand-year-old temple.”

### 3. Ordinary visit, pilgrimage and festival are distinct situations

UNESCO and China ICH sources document formal fairs, smaller ceremonies, pilgrimage networks and temple-based practice. The official temple site advertises a seasonal daily morning worship/recitation in the south-axis Tianhou Hall and describes it as open to believers. This proves that the institution publishes a recurring activity; it does not prove access, observer zones, late entry or filming rights for a non-participant.

A future article may use a three-mode recognition framework only after local review:

| Mode | Evidence-safe description | Still needs local confirmation |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary public visit | public halls or courts may coexist with individual worship and tour groups | exact route, optionality of actions, hall boundaries and photo rules |
| Organized worship/pilgrimage | community groups may have offerings, images, banners and coordinated movement | where casual visitors may stand and whether observation is welcome |
| Named festival/rite | organizer notices may establish a date, programme and controlled area | public access, capacity controls, recording permissions and route on that date |

### 4. Ferry evidence is necessary but not a passport workflow

The current official ferry portal publishes a year-round pattern, fare components and the requirement to present valid identification. It also says weather, maintenance and emergency dispatch can override the timetable. A July 2026 transport-authority record identifies Putian Zhongmei Ferry Co. as the licensed passenger operator through 23 April 2029. A February 2026 transport report confirms that the operator was using online and offline channels, staffed windows, self-service terminals, an official account and terminal screens during Spring Festival operations.

The only direct passport-specific evidence located is an operator notice dated 29 April 2024 and republished by Dongnan Net: during the named limit-control arrangement, offline windows were open only to island residents and passport holders. That statement can confirm the 2024 arrangement. It cannot be generalized into the current ordinary-date workflow, and it does not explain whether a foreign passport works online, which document fields are accepted, whether transliterated names must match exactly, or how check-in handles a failed booking.

### 5. Accessibility aspirations are not a verified travel chain

A 2022 Meizhou government work report says the island intended to create an accessible model street and improve tourism accessibility. That is evidence of a policy/project direction, not a route audit. No current official source located in this revision confirms step-free boarding at Wenjia, assistance booking, the island-side terminal path, an accessible transfer to the ancestral temple, continuous step-free movement through the west/south axes, rest points or accessible toilets.

For publication, every link in the chain must be confirmed. “The island has accessibility improvements” cannot be converted into “the temple is wheelchair accessible.”
