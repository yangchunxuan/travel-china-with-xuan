## Direct answer

Beijing has public-facing robot exhibition and retail experiences, but the broader humanoid-robot industry is not a freely visitable district. Current official material supports two conditional visitor propositions in Beijing E-Town: **Robot World**, which publishes a structured reservation route and an explicit workaround for foreign passport holders, and **Robot Mall**, which operates as a ticketed public experience but does not publish a complete foreign-passport booking path. Production platforms, innovation centres, data-training bases and factory facilities are industry or institutional spaces unless a current official event notice expressly opens them.

The editorial risk is high because official names and functions overlap. Robot World and Robot Mall are promoted by closely related E-Town entities and may be complementary, renamed or evolving products. They should not be sold as two guaranteed independent stops until the operator confirms the current venue names, addresses, ticket products and booking rules.

## Why this is not yet a standalone visitor page

1. The headline promise would imply a stable public ‘robot district’, while the verified offer is two operator-managed experiences plus mostly non-public industrial sites.
2. The clearest foreign-visitor route relies on a Chinese-ID holder making the booking. That is a material access condition, not a minor footnote.
3. Official names, addresses and product boundaries are changing quickly. Current reports are useful evidence for 2026, but weak foundations for an evergreen route.
4. Factory and laboratory access reported in news or delegations cannot be generalized to independent tourists.
5. A visitor page could accidentally invite people to photograph or enter industrial premises that are not public.

## Publication gate

Before central converts this into an article or module, obtain written or recorded confirmation from the current operator covering:

- whether Robot World and Robot Mall are distinct bookable venues on the desired date;
- each exact Chinese name, address and public entrance;
- whether an individual foreign visitor can reserve without a mainland ID card;
- accepted passport details, phone numbers and payment methods;
- whether the original passport is required at admission;
- current opening days, entry windows and same-day availability;
- photography restrictions and whether demonstrations are scheduled or contingent;
- cancellation procedure and contact channel in case the mini-program rejects foreign details.

## Safe editorial framing if the gate is met

Lead with ‘what a visitor can actually enter’, not with an industrial-development tour. Build one confirmed public stop, one backup indoor attraction unrelated to robots, and an explicit warning that nearby R&D and production buildings are not visitor sites. Keep industry scale, forecasts and policy ambitions out of the practical route unless independently verified and directly relevant.
