## 1. Gate decision

This revision closes the conceptual question but does **not** close the operational and image-rights gates required for a publishable first-visit article.

What can now be stated safely:

- Anshun Dixi has relationships with the wider study of Nuo culture, ritual and so-called military Nuo, but “Dixi equals Nuo opera” is not an uncontested classification. The national intangible-cultural-heritage register lists Nuo opera as `IV-89` and Anshun Dixi separately as `IV-90`; scholarship records opposing positions.
- The observable forehead-worn mask is well documented. No official or reliable academic source reviewed here establishes one unique historical reason for why that position first developed. Provincial representative practitioner Gu Jiashun's explanation — respect for the character being portrayed — may be used only with his name and as a current practitioner interpretation.
- A July 2026 national-government source says Tianlong's Yanwutang presents timed, roughly ten-minute Dixi excerpts every day. It does not provide performance times or cancellation conditions.

## 2. Direct answer and narrative line

The eventual article should answer the title in its first 150 English words:

> The mask is not being worn incorrectly. In Anshun Dixi, a dark veil withdraws the performer's own face while the carved *lianzi* sits above it, and character emerges through the carving, voice, gong and drum, weapons and full-body movement. The historical reason this arrangement first developed cannot be reduced to one proven answer. Practitioner Gu Jiashun explains it as respect for the role; that is a named living-practice interpretation, not a settled origin theory.

The visitor-facing narrative is:

`the face above the face → what mask, veil, voice, percussion and movement each do → why military stories are literature rather than battlefield records → why Dixi and Nuo are related but not interchangeable labels → how a village practice differs from a ten-minute tourism excerpt → what Tianlong can teach a first-time visitor → what to do when the current performance cannot be verified`

The article must not judge one setting as “real” and another as “fake.” A village ritual sequence, a public tourism excerpt, an invited festival performance and contemporary choreography may share people and forms while differing in purpose, duration, audience, space and permission.

## 3. Dixi and Nuo: the corrected boundary

The safest short formulation is:

> Anshun Dixi is often discussed within the wider field of Nuo culture or “military Nuo,” but the relationship is not an uncontested equals sign. China's national ICH register gives Nuo opera and Anshun Dixi separate project numbers, and scholars have long disagreed over whether Dixi should be classified as Nuo opera. For a visitor, the useful conclusion is that they are related research traditions while Anshun Dixi retains its own Tunpu village setting, heroic-war narratives, forehead-worn *lianzi* and ritual–theatre structure.

Evidence boundaries:

1. **Administrative classification is evidence of distinct official project identity, not proof of total cultural separation.** The national register lists Nuo opera as `IV-89` and Anshun Dixi as `IV-90`.
2. **The scholarly category is disputed.** Recent scholarship cites both Fan Zengru's “Anshun Dixi Is Not Nuo Opera” and Tuo Xiuming's “Chinese Military Nuo — Guizhou Dixi.” This permits the article to report disagreement, not to declare either camp the only answer.
3. **Ritual and Nuo relationships are supportable.** Zhang Dinggui's ritual study connects Dixi with a Nuo-ritual prototype and records opening, performance and closing sequences. That does not make every Nuo form equivalent to Dixi.
4. **Forehead-wearing is not an exclusive diagnostic test.** The national ICH page for Linwu Nuo opera also documents masks placed on the forehead. The article must not say “all Nuo masks cover the face, while only Dixi masks sit on the forehead.”

## 4. Revised outline for a future approved article

Target if the gates later close: 2,150–2,400 English words, 9–10 main H2s, with at least 60% devoted to observation, choice, visit order and failure recovery.

### Opening — The face above the face

- Describe only the verifiable construction: dark face veil, carved face above it, battle skirt, back flags and stage weapon.
- Give the direct answer above.
- State that Tianlong can be a low-threshold introduction only when the same-day performance is confirmed; it is not a substitute for a complete village ritual.

### H2 — What exactly is Anshun Dixi?

- Establish the `IV-90` national ICH project and Anshun Culture Center as protection unit.
- Put four evidence layers on the page: documented history, living practice, literary story, tourism presentation.
- Explain why “an ancient mask dance” is too vague to guide observation.

### H2 — Is Dixi the same thing as Nuo opera?

- Use the corrected relationship above.
- Show separate register identities and scholarly disagreement.
- Tell readers to keep the local name *Anshun Dixi* rather than replacing it with a broad label.

### H2 — Does it come unchanged from a Ming military camp?

- The military-colony context and martial narrative are supportable.
- Exact formation time lacks direct documentation and has competing proposals.
- Correct the misconception: military context does not prove every movement is an unchanged Ming drill.

### H2 — Why is the carved face on the forehead?

- Observation 1: locate the veil, the carved *lianzi*, the performer's voice and the whole costume as one role-making system.
- Say that the unique historical origin is **unknown**.
- Attribute “respect for the role” to Gu Jiashun only.
- Do not infer an origin from plausible functions such as breathing, sight lines or combat convenience.

### H2 — What should you read in a mask before reaching for a colour chart?

- Observation 2: silhouette, helmet and ear-wing structure, age/gender/warrior type, carving and attachment.
- Do not import a universal Peking-opera colour code.
- Do not touch masks, storage boxes or scripts; follow the rules of the specific display.

### H2 — How do lead voice, chorus, gong and drum move the story?

- Observation 3: track who initiates a sung passage, who answers and when percussion marks a turn to movement.
- Traditional descriptions record seven- and ten-character verse, one lead voice with group response, and one gong plus one drum; allow troupe variation.
- Do not promise English surtitles, interpretation or a plot sheet.

### H2 — Are the generals history or literature?

- Observation 4: identify opposing camps and the compression of a conflict rather than trying to verify each episode as history.
- Yang-family generals, Yue-family generals, Three Kingdoms and *Investiture of the Gods* material has passed through historical romance, storytelling and local scripts.
- A historical name is not proof that the staged action is a historical record.

### H2 — Why does the whole body matter more than a close-up?

- Observation 5: battle skirt, back flags, weapon path, formation and transitions between singing and fighting.
- The veil reduces ordinary facial expression, so the carving, sound and body work together.
- Avoid a tight portrait-only viewing strategy and never obstruct the performers for a photograph.

### H2 — What does a ten-minute Tianlong excerpt keep and omit?

- The July 2026 official report supports a roughly ten-minute timed excerpt at Yanwutang, but not exact times.
- Compare this with documented village sequences such as opening the box, performance and closing the box, without promising outsiders access.
- The excerpt can retain masks, voice, percussion, martial action and a plot fragment; it cannot represent every ritual, long script or village relationship.

### H2 — Is Tianlong worth the detour for a first encounter?

- Yes for a visitor who has confirmed a performance and wants a public, legible entry into form and Tunpu setting.
- Lower priority for a traveller who requires long-form drama, guaranteed English interpretation or an unconfirmed narrow time window.
- Never encourage visitors to enter another village, request a private opening ritual or treat residents as performers.

### H2 — How should a half-day work when the performance is uncertain?

1. Ask the operator the seven gate questions for the exact date.
2. At the visitor centre, reconfirm the day's venue and whether the listed old/new Yanwutang is the active performance space.
3. If a verified display is open, learn the mask and plot vocabulary first.
4. Watch one public excerpt from a lawful audience position, following sound, movement and formation rather than recording the whole show.
5. Return the excerpt to the public village context without entering homes.
6. If no performance is confirmed, downgrade the visit to public-space observation or choose another plan; do not hunt for a private village performance.

### H2 — Do not assume

- Dixi is simply another name for every form of Nuo opera.
- All Nuo masks cover the face or only Dixi uses the forehead.
- One proven historical explanation exists for the forehead position.
- The performance is an unchanged Ming military drill.
- Heroic names turn a romance or script into primary historical evidence.
- “Living fossil” is an academic finding that the tradition never changed.
- A ten-minute tourism excerpt is a complete village ritual.
- A ticket automatically grants portrait, full-video or publication rights.

### H2 — What is the recovery plan?

- No confirmed performance: use only a verified public display, current notice and public village-space line; otherwise skip Tianlong rather than promise Dixi.
- Display closed: do not invent an exhibition stop or substitute an unrelated mask museum.
- Venue ambiguity: ask staff to identify the current Yanwutang; do not navigate to the documented old hall and assume the show is there.
- No English material: use a pre-saved role/sound/action framework; do not guess spoken meaning.
- Photography unclear: keep the camera away and observe; admission does not imply publication permission.
