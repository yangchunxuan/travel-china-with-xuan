## Direct answer proposed for the future opening

The first 150 words should answer the decision before giving background:

- Choose **Chadong (today's Biancheng Town)** to read the novel's border geography: river crossing, ferry, old river street, and the relationship between Chadong and the more intimate Bixiju setting discussed in literary scholarship.
- Choose **Fenghuang** to understand Shen Congwen's birthplace, former residence, memorial landscape, and the later tourism reception of his writing.
- Choose **both** only if the traveller wants both literary geography and author biography. Neither town is a one-to-one, fully preserved map of the novel.

This is an editorial decision rule grounded in the evidence below, not an official designation.

## Current search-intent snapshot

Google result samples checked on 2026-08-13 for English and Chinese variants of “Where is *Border Town*?”, “Fenghuang or Chadong?”, and “Shen Congwen former residence” showed three recurring reader problems:

1. travel pages frequently collapse Fenghuang, Shen's birthplace, into the novel's setting;
2. official Chadong promotion often uses “prototype” while also presenting reconstructed characters and newer literary installations;
3. few results explain that scholarship distinguishes Chadong town from Bixiju, where much of the novel's action is situated.

The proposed page therefore owns a **place-choice and evidence-layer task**, not a plot summary or a generic ancient-town comparison.

## Narrative spine

Begin with two riverside images the traveller can genuinely encounter—a ferry crossing at Chadong and Shen Congwen's courtyard in Fenghuang—then reveal why both are connected to *Border Town* but answer different questions. Move from the novel's spatial vocabulary to Shen's documented life, then expose the modern tourism layer in both towns. Finish with two short on-site reading sequences and a decision rule for one place or both.

Narrative turns to preserve:

1. “Fenghuang is famous because of Shen” does **not** mean “Fenghuang is the novel's prototype.”
2. “Chadong is the model” does **not** mean every Cui Cui object on site is historical or described as a fixed real-world object in the novel.
3. The contemporary literary landscape is not fake merely because it is new; it is evidence of reception and place-making, not evidence of 1930s material survival.

## Detailed article outline

### 1. Opening: two places, two different answers

- Future scene anchor: Chadong ferry cable/boat and Fenghuang former-residence courtyard; no invented weather, sound, dialogue, or personal visit.
- Give the direct choice above immediately.
- Define the page's four labels: documented life, literary setting, later memorialization, tourism presentation.

### 2. Why does the title cause so much confusion?

- Explain the shared “湘西/Shen Congwen” association without treating western Hunan as a single town.
- Note that official tourism language uses “原型地/prototype” for Chadong while Fenghuang markets Shen's home and adaptations.
- Reader takeaway: ask “prototype of what?”—geography, author biography, or present-day scenic branding.

### 3. What does the novel actually let us locate?

- Paraphrase only the minimum spatial logic; do not reproduce a modern translation.
- Use Jishou University scholarship to distinguish Chadong from Bixiju and to explain the novel's layered natural, domestic, and town spaces.
- State the limit: literature can transform and combine observed places; it does not establish an exact visitor map.
- Memorable discovery 1: the novel's most important action is not simply “inside Chadong town.”

### 4. What makes Chadong the stronger literary-geography choice?

- Locate the town at the Hunan–Guizhou–Chongqing meeting area.
- Explain why river, ferry, bank-to-bank movement, and old river street help a reader understand the story's border logic.
- Separate long-lived transport practice from the exact boat, ticket system, staging, and visitor volume of 2026.
- Do not claim that every surviving building is from the novel's period.

### 5. What should you inspect before accepting a “Border Town” object as old?

- Read signs for words such as “复建,” “模拟复原,” “景观,” and construction dates.
- Use the government-documented reconstructed Cui Cui residence as the clearest example.
- Compare the 1993 imitation-style cliff lettering with the author-lettering installation publicized in 2026.
- Memorable discovery 2: even the most photographed “Border Town” words have their own modern chronology.

### 6. A 90-minute Chadong literary-reading sequence

This is an interpretive order, not a transport or ticket guide:

1. start at a reliable town/river orientation sign and identify the provincial junction;
2. observe the ferry landing and what movement it connects before riding anything;
3. walk the old river street and distinguish fabric, repairs, and themed signs;
4. examine one explicitly reconstructed Cui Cui element as tourism reception;
5. end at the lettering/interpretive display and ask which date and author it documents.

Failure recovery: if the ferry is suspended or the bank is crowded, retain the literary task by using the landing, river alignment, street, and dated interpretive signs; never promise a crossing.

### 7. Why does Fenghuang still matter to a *Border Town* reader?

- Establish Shen's birth and early-life connection through the Hunan Archives biography.
- Establish the former residence as protected material heritage through the Hunan Cultural Relics Bureau.
- Explain that author biography can illuminate recurring regional observation without turning every local street into a novel location.
- Memorable discovery 3: Fenghuang's strongest claim is the writer's life and reception, not a direct one-town substitution for Chadong.

### 8. A 90-minute Fenghuang author-and-reception sequence

1. begin at the former residence and read the site label before the room displays;
2. identify what the museum attributes to Shen's household/life and what is later exhibition interpretation;
3. use the surrounding street and Tuojiang view as geographic context, not proof of a fictional episode;
4. note later *Border Town* branding or performance advertising as reception evidence;
5. finish with the heritage-route framing only if the signage is current.

Failure recovery: if the residence is closed or fully booked, do not replace it with an unverified “old house.” Use its exterior heritage plaque, a current official exhibition or museum if available, and the river/street context; state that the core interior evidence was missed.

### 9. Chadong, Fenghuang, or both: the traveller matrix

| Traveller | Better choice | Why | What they give up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read the novel and want spatial logic | Chadong | ferry, border position, Chadong/Bixiju discussion | detailed author-home context |
| Interested in Shen's biography or museums | Fenghuang | protected former residence and memorial landscape | strongest literary-geography comparison |
| Collecting literary places, with time for two distinct stops | Both | separates setting from life/reception | more transfer time; no need to repeat generic old-town sightseeing |
| Has not read the book and wants only an attractive old town | Skip this literary detour or choose by a separate destination guide | this page cannot justify either place purely as scenery | literary layer will be thin |

Do not insert intercity timetables, hotel advice, or a Zhangjiajie–Fenghuang itinerary.

### 10. Do not mistake these four things

- “Shen was born here” ≠ “the novel occurs here.”
- “prototype” ≠ “every scene and building can be matched.”
- “named for the novel” ≠ “that municipal name existed when the novel was written.”
- “themed performance” ≠ “historical evidence,” while it may still show later reception.

### 11. Photography, text, and cultural sensitivity

- Do not use film, television, stage-production, or modern-illustration stills without rights.
- Do not photograph residents as picturesque “characters” without consent.
- Photograph plaques together with the object they identify; avoid using a generic stilt-house image to prove age.
- Paraphrase the novel and cite an edition; do not copy a copyrighted modern English or Korean translation.
- A named Hunan/湘西 local reviewer should check Chadong/Bixiju terminology and any Sani/Miao/Tujia cultural references introduced later.

### 12. Final verification and graceful fallback

- Recheck former-residence opening, reservations, ferry operation, bank access, performance status, filming restrictions, and crowd controls within seven days of publication and again before travel.
- If current operator evidence cannot be found, keep the article's historical choice logic but remove precise operating promises.
- End naturally: Homeground can help fit the literary stop into a wider trip, without selling a fixed-price product.
