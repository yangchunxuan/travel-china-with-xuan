## Direct answer proposed for the future opening

The future opening should reassure without turning living belief into spectacle:

- A non-believer can visit the public areas of Meizhou Mazu Ancestral Temple respectfully. The visitor does not need to imitate worship, burn incense, or decide whether the belief is “true.”
- First identify whether the day is an **ordinary public visit**, a **pilgrimage/worship day**, or a **major festival/procession**. Space, crowding, movement, photography, and the amount a casual visitor can understand differ sharply.
- Read the **west and south axes** as different material histories. Official temple records say the visible west-axis complex was largely rebuilt from the 1980s to 1994, while the south-axis complex was built from 1998 to 2002. Do not call the whole visible complex an untouched Song-dynasty temple.
- Use the exhibition/interpretive material before watching ritual action; stand back, follow posted signs and staff direction, and ask before photographing people, offerings, or ceremonies.

Etiquette points are Homeground risk-reduction proposals until a named local reviewer and current posted rules confirm them.

## Detailed article outline

### 1. Opening: are you entering an attraction, a temple, or a pilgrimage destination?

- Concrete but non-fictional visual: a public direction sign, an incense/worship area, and visitors moving along the same axis for different purposes.

### 2. Who is Mazu—and what wording respects both evidence and belief?

- Follow UNESCO's formulation: Mazu is believed to have lived in the tenth century on Meizhou Island, helped local people, and died in a rescue account.
- Identify Lin Mo/Lin Moniang as names used within the tradition, with EN/ZH/KO terminology to be reviewed.

### 4. What are the three visit modes?

| Mode | What the visitor may encounter | Best behaviour | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary public visit | open halls, individual worship, tour groups, exhibition/interpretive spaces | use the low-interference route; pause where worship is active | best first visit for careful understanding |
| Pilgrimage or organized worship | groups carrying images/offerings, formal bows, incense, dedicated tables, staff coordination | do not cut through the group or copy ritual for a photo; ask staff where to stand | observe only if space and permission allow |
| Major festival/procession | formal rites, large groups, controlled movement, performances, crowd/transport measures | prioritize safety and community purpose; accept restricted views and photography | choose deliberately, not as a guaranteed “better” visit |

### 6. A first route for a non-believer: learn before you obstruct

Provisional low-interference order, pending a current map and local walk-through:

1. **Entrance/orientation:** identify west axis, south axis, exits, active event zones, and posted photo restrictions.
2. **Exhibition or interpretation first:** learn the belief vocabulary, temple genealogy, and ritual categories before looking at action.
3. **Outer court/threshold:** observe where queues, offerings, and group routes form; do not stand on the central movement line.
4. **One principal hall from the permitted visitor zone:** read the hall name and object labels; keep the visit short if worship is active.
5. **West-axis material-history stop:** locate one dated rebuilding/conservation marker.
6. **Quiet exterior/viewpoint finish:** review the difference among tradition, current practice, and modern construction.

Do not publish exact compass turns or doorway sequences until the current official map and on-site manager verify them.

### 7. Must a non-believer burn incense, bow, donate, or make a wish?

- Proposed answer: no participation should be presented as compulsory for a public cultural visit.
- If a visitor genuinely wishes to participate, ask temple staff how and where; do not improvise by copying another group.
- Never turn another person's prayer, offering, or ritual gesture into a prop.
- Do not prescribe a universal number of bows, incense sticks, doorway side, or threshold step without a site-specific official rule.
- A donation is not a photo fee and should never be suggested as purchasing access to worshippers.

### 8. What can you learn by watching without interrupting?

- Observe spatial roles: where offerings are placed, where organized groups gather, how staff make a route, and which halls receive sustained attention.
- Use labels and public interpretation to name actions; if the meaning is not documented, describe only what is visibly occurring without inventing symbolism.
- The official temple lists a daily morning worship/recitation activity with seasonal times, but the final article must verify whether and where a non-participant may observe.
- Memorable discovery 3: activity frequency does not convert a ritual into a scheduled tourist show.

### 9. How is pilgrimage different from ordinary sightseeing?

- UNESCO describes smaller worship ceremonies and community practice beyond major fairs.
- China ICH research identifies temple genealogy, direct/indirect branch relationships, and pilgrimage as a community network.
- Explain that pilgrims may arrive with a deity image, offerings, or an organized ritual objective; a casual visitor's job is not to join the route uninvited.
- Do not photograph identifying faces, group banners, deity images, or offerings at close range without permission.

### 10. Should a first-timer go during a major Mazu festival?

- Annual tradition centers on lunar third-month day 23 and ninth-month day 9; exact Gregorian dates and programs change.
- Explain trade-off: more formal ritual and community participation, but greater ferry/crowd controls, restricted sightlines, and photography sensitivity.
- Recommend an ordinary day for a reader prioritizing space and interpretation; recommend a festival only to someone who accepts crowd/transport uncertainty and observes community priority.
- Never promise a procession, sea rite, route, performance, or unrestricted access from a recurring lunar date alone.

### 11. Photography: what is safe to assume, and what is not?

- No comprehensive current public site-wide photo rule was found; therefore the page must defer to each hall/event sign and staff instruction.
- Low-risk editorial baseline: architecture from public space where permitted; no flash; no drone; no close identifiable worshipper, offering, or ritual image without permission; no blocking movement; no posing as worship.
- “No flash/no drone” remains advice, not a claimed posted ban, until current rules confirm it.
- For an organized rite, request organizer and depicted-person permission; temple/property permission alone may not cover people.

### 12. What does the ferry change about a cultural visit?

- The island trip is capacity- and weather-dependent; a temple itinerary cannot ignore the final return sailing and on-site dispatch.
- Record the current official ferry schedule and fare only in a dated service box, not in the timeless cultural explanation.
- The official service page says weather, maintenance, and emergency dispatch can override the schedule.
- Foreign-passport online/offline workflow needs direct operator confirmation; holiday media says passport holders used a staffed window during a named period, which cannot be generalized.

Failure recovery:

- ferry delay/cancellation: do not compress the temple into a rushed dash; use the operator's rebooking/dispatch instruction and preserve return margin;
- major ritual blocks the planned hall: move to the exhibition/exterior and let the group pass;
- photography unclear: put the camera away and use written observation;
- hall closed: do not substitute the film park or a newer statue as equivalent historical evidence;
- overwhelming crowd: leave the active axis and use a quieter public interpretation area.

### 13. Who should choose an ordinary day, festival day, or skip?

| Traveller | Best choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| First cultural visit, non-believer | ordinary weekday/non-festival date after current check | easier to read space and ask questions |

| Traveller uncomfortable around active worship | exhibition/exterior route or skip | respectful distance is better than treating ritual as a show |
| Mobility/crowd-sensitive traveller | current accessible route outside major dates | slopes, stairs, ferry and crowd controls need verification |

### 14. Do not mistake these things

- faith biography ≠ fully documented secular biography;
- ICH inscription ≠ UNESCO validation of theological truth;
- ancestral temple ≠ every building is original early fabric;
- Song-style architecture ≠ Song-dynasty construction;
- daily ritual ≠ tourist performance;
- festival date ≠ guaranteed visitor access;
- ferry ticket bundle ≠ a fixed cultural-site price forever.
