## Direct answer proposed for the future opening

The first 150 words should establish the route and the evidence rule:

- **Lu Xun's former residence (Zhou Family New Taimen), ancestral residence, and Sanwei Study** are documented life/education sites within a protected group.
- **Baicao Garden** is a real family-site landscape that Lu Xun later reshaped through memory in “From Baicao Garden to Sanwei Study”; the essay is not a neutral inventory of every plant and object visible today.
- **Kong Yiji is a literary character**, not a former resident waiting to be matched to a statue. Today's Xianheng Hotel and “Kong Yiji” streetscape are commercial and interpretive layers, even where the hotel name and Shaoxing food culture have historical roots.
- A useful 2–3 hour walk should move from family and school evidence to memoir, then to museum interpretation, and leave modern literary commerce until last.

## Current search-intent snapshot

Google result samples checked on 2026-08-13 for “Shaoxing Lu Xun walk,” “Baicao Garden Sanwei Study,” “Kong Yiji Xianheng Hotel,” and Chinese equivalents showed:

## Narrative spine

Use the physical walk to change evidence categories. Start in a documented family residence, cross into an actual schoolroom, step into a garden remembered decades later in prose, then enter a modern museum that explains the life. Only after those anchors should the visitor meet Kong Yiji statues, themed shop names, and a recreated tavern scene. The walk becomes more interesting—not less—when the reader knows which layer changed at each threshold.

Narrative turns to preserve:

1. The garden and school are real places, but the famous essay is crafted memory rather than a live transcript of childhood.
2. A protected compound can contain preserved fabric, repaired fabric, restored settings, and a modern museum at the same time.
3. Kong Yiji's enormous physical visibility in today's street does not turn him into a documented resident.

## Detailed article outline

### 1. Opening: every doorway changes the kind of evidence

- Concrete non-fictional sequence: a Taimen threshold, a small study room, a garden wall/well marker, a modern shop sign.
- Give the direct answer and route logic immediately.
- Introduce four visible labels the reader will use: lived here, remembered this, invented this, staged this later.

### 2. What exactly is included in “Lu Xun Native Place”?

- Use the NDRC and official memorial sources to identify the former residence, ancestral residence, Sanwei Study, Baicao Garden, memorial museum, and adjacent interpretive/commercial spaces.
- Explain why an attraction boundary is not one historical date.
- Tell readers to photograph the current site plan because entry sequences and construction zones can change.

### 3. First stop: what does Zhou Family Old Taimen explain?

- Treat the ancestral residence as family/social context, not automatically as Lu Xun's birthplace room.
- Observe Taimen organization, official room labels, ancestral/family function, and protection plaques.
- Distinguish a documented building use from a later room arrangement.
- Keep architectural interpretation narrow unless a conservation report is obtained.

### 4. Second stop: why is Sanwei Study stronger evidence than a school scene in a movie?

- Establish that Sanwei Study was in the Shou family compound and served as Lu Xun's school.
- Observe the room's scale, orientation, inscription/painting, desk labels, and the relationship between schoolroom and family residence.
- Introduce the “早” desk cautiously: current official reporting says an attributed desk came from museum storage into display, but the final body must use the museum's provenance wording exactly.
- Memorable discovery 1: the room is a life site; the most famous desk anecdote still needs object-level attribution.

### 5. Third stop: what is historically present at Zhou Family New Taimen?

- Establish Lu Xun's birth and youth connection through the official site account.
- Use the 1918 sale and 1920–1922 redevelopment record to explain why only specified portions survived while the wider compound changed.
- Look for labels that separate surviving rooms, later restoration, room reconstructions, and furnishings.
- Never write “the whole house is exactly as Lu Xun left it.”

### 6. Fourth stop: is Baicao Garden a real place or a literary one?

- Answer: both, but in different senses.
- The garden belongs to the documented family-site landscape; the famous essay is a retrospective literary treatment.
- Use the original Chinese essay only for minimal paraphrase and a cited publication context; do not reproduce a modern translation.
- Observe the stone well rim, low wall, garden extent, labels, and any stated original/repaired status rather than matching every named plant.
- Memorable discovery 2: “this place appears in a memoir” is stronger and more precise than “everything here is preserved from the essay.”

### 7. What did the 1953 museum—and later rebuilding—add?

- Establish the memorial museum's 1953 founding.
- Explain the 2003 removal of the older exhibition hall, restoration of the New Taimen environment, and construction of a new museum as an institutional history layer.
- Show how a modern museum can supply biography, manuscripts, editions, photographs, and chronology that the old rooms cannot.
- Do not imply that a modern museum building is a historical residence.

### 8. How should you read memoir beside a real room?

- Give a reusable method: label the author's age at the event; date of writing; genre; physical object/site evidence; later conservation change.
- Use “From Baicao Garden to Sanwei Study” as memoir/essay, not a contemporaneous diary.
- Tell the traveller to compare what the text foregrounds—childhood attention and remembered contrast—with what the museum documents.
- Avoid a plant checklist and nostalgic “textbook came alive” cliché.

### 9. Where does fiction begin?

- Introduce *Kong Yiji* as a short story first published in 1919 and its tavern as a literary setting.
- State explicitly that Kong Yiji is not to be treated as a verified local resident; possible inspirations discussed in later commentary do not collapse the character into one historical person.
- “Lu Town” and related fictional figures elsewhere in Lu Xun's work require the same rule.
- Memorable discovery 3: an identifiable local social world can inform fiction without making the fictional cast a census.

### 10. What is the present Xianheng Hotel evidence of?

- Separate at least three claims: the name/business history, the fictional tavern setting, and today's restaurant/visitor staging.
- Use the current official/local source only to document how tourism presents the hotel and Kong Yiji statue.
- Require a direct company or heritage source before claiming a continuous original interior or exact late-Qing layout.
- Food, wine, statue, and counter can be an enjoyable reception layer; they are not a preserved scene with Kong Yiji.

### 11. Why do the new shop signs matter?

- Use the 2025 Zhejiang culture report to document current literary-IP shops and deliberately constructed scenes.
- Treat them as evidence that Lu Xun's writing is being commercialized and interpreted in the present.
- Read the explanatory notes the project says it places in shops; verify on site.
- Do not make a moralistic “authentic/fake” binary: ask whether each presentation identifies its date and function.

### 12. A 2–3 hour walk that keeps the layers in order

Provisional route, pending current entrance/construction verification:

1. **Orientation, 10 minutes:** current site map, entry/exit, closures, and photography signs.
2. **Ancestral residence, 20–25 minutes:** family structure and room labels.
3. **Sanwei Study, 20–25 minutes:** school evidence and attributed-object caution.
4. **Former residence/New Taimen, 25–35 minutes:** surviving/restored-room distinctions.
5. **Baicao Garden, 15–20 minutes:** material details versus remembered prose.
6. **Memorial museum, 40–55 minutes:** biography and source objects.
7. **Xianheng/IP streetscape, 15–25 minutes:** fiction and modern reception; optional food stop sits outside the core time.

The final body must validate walking order, one-way controls, construction barriers, and total dwell time with a current map and local walk-through.

Failure recovery:

- reservation unavailable: use only publicly accessible street context and reschedule; do not call exterior viewing equivalent to the core visit;
- a room is closed: use the museum chronology/object display and do not invent interior details;
- Baicao Garden crowded: focus on two labelled material details rather than trying to recreate a prose checklist;
- construction alters the route: preserve the evidence order even if the physical order changes;
- Xianheng closed/too busy: end at the exterior or skip it; the literary walk remains complete.

### 13. Who is this walk for—and who can skip it?

| Traveller | Recommendation | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Has read Lu Xun or remembers school texts | full 2–3 hour sequence | reveals how place, memory, and reception diverge |
| First encounter with Lu Xun | former residence + Sanwei + museum; read one short text before/after | avoids a quotation scavenger hunt |
| Architecture/heritage interest | prioritize Taimen labels, conservation chronology, and museum | strongest material evidence |
| Wants only a restaurant/photo stop | Xianheng can be visited separately; this deep walk may be skippable | commercial scene alone cannot answer the literary task |
| Severe crowd/mobility constraint | museum and one accessible life site after current access check | preserves evidence without forcing every threshold |

### 14. Do not mistake these things

- memoir ≠ real-time diary;
- fictional character ≠ historical resident;
- protected site ≠ every surface untouched;
- attributed desk ≠ confirmed autograph until the label/provenance says so;
- restored environment ≠ surviving original compound in full;
- modern Xianheng/IP street ≠ the original condition of the fictional tavern.

### 15. Photography, copyright, and cultural handling

- No long modern English/Korean translation, textbook illustration, film/TV still, or stage image without licence.
- Interior and object photography follows current museum signs; no flash/tripod claim without direct rule.
- Do not climb, touch, or pose on garden walls/well features or school furniture.
- Do not photograph students or residents as “Kong Yiji characters” without consent.
- Captions must identify preserved, restored, reconstructed, displayed, or commercial status.
- A named Shaoxing Lu Xun memorial/local literary reviewer should verify room sequence, object attribution, Xianheng wording, and natural EN/ZH/KO names.

### 16. Final verification and close

- Recheck regular hours, reservation/passport path, free/paid status, construction closures, one-way circulation, accessibility, object rotation, Xianheng operation, and photography rules within seven days of publication and before travel.
- Dynamic failure should shorten the route, not blur evidence labels.
- End with a natural option for Homeground to place the walk into a wider trip; no transport duplication or fixed-price product pitch.
