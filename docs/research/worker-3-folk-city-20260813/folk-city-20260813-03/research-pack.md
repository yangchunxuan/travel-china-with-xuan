## Direct answer proposed for the future opening

The future opening should make the choice within 150 words:

- Make **Pu Songling Memorial Museum and the former-residence compound** the core. The museum was established on the residence site; they are not two unrelated attractions.
- Add **Liuquan and the cemetery** only after confirming current access and understanding that the familiar “tea and story collecting” account needs a folklore/interpretation label.
- Add **Liaozhai City or another themed attraction** only if the traveller wants to see modern adaptation and entertainment. Its sets, fox-spirit imagery, light shows, or immersive performances do not document Pu's daily life.
- Treat **Liaozhai liqu (聊斋俚曲)** as a distinct sung literary/musical tradition, not background music for a “ghost house.” A performance must be verified for the actual date.

## Narrative spine

Open with the contrast between a northern rural courtyard and a modern fantasy gate. Follow one verifiable person—Pu Songling—from his home site to his writings, then turn sideways into the lesser-known sung corpus of Liaozhai liqu. Only after the reader has a historical and literary baseline should the article enter the theme park, where the question becomes “how is Liaozhai being reimagined now?” End with a modular half-day sequence and a clear skip/add rule.

Narrative turns to preserve:

1. The former residence is historically important, but official records also say it was restored; “protected” does not mean every visible surface is untouched.
2. “Liaozhai” names more than the famous prose collection: the documented liqu tradition has its own texts, tunes, transmission, and losses.
3. Modern fantasy staging can be enjoyable and culturally revealing, but it is evidence of reception, not Qing-period life.

## Detailed article outline

### 1. Opening: the least useful question is “where is the ghost house?”

- Concrete but non-fictional image pair: low rural courtyard roofline versus themed fantasy entrance.
- Deliver the direct choice above.
- Tell readers the page will sort one author, one residence/museum, one sung tradition, two attached sites, and one modern attraction layer.

### 2. Are the former residence and memorial museum separate places?

- Explain that the memorial museum was established on the basis of the former residence in 1980.
- Identify the integrated compound before discussing route order.
- Warn that published courtyard/exhibition-room counts differ by date; do not use a number as timeless orientation.
- Traveller action: photograph/read the current site plan rather than follow a stale blog map.

### 3. What can this courtyard actually prove about Pu Songling?

- Establish Pu's 1640–1715 life dates and Zichuan origin from official records.
- Describe only verifiable northern rural courtyard characteristics documented by the institution.
- Use the 1954 restoration and 1980 museum expansion chronology to set the authenticity boundary.
- On site, look for construction labels, restoration records, room-function evidence, and the distinction between protected fabric and exhibition furnishing.
- Memorable discovery 1: “former residence” and “museum” overlap, but “former” is not a licence to call every display original.

### 4. Which museum objects bring the writer closer than a ghost statue?

- Prioritize a dated portrait, manuscript/author-edited manuscript, early or significant editions, correspondence, and research history if currently displayed.
- The 2026 National Museum exhibition proves these categories exist in institutional collections; it does not prove each object is on display in Zibo.
- Require the local museum's current gallery list before naming a must-see object.
- Tell readers to read object labels for owner, date, original/facsimile status, and provenance.

### 5. How should a first-time reader understand *Liaozhai Zhiyi* without a plot dump?

- One concise literary frame: a collection of short classical-Chinese narratives associated with Pu Songling, not a single haunted-house story.
- Use two or three themes only if supported by a scholarly/museum source during drafting; avoid “all stories are about ghosts.”
- No long quotation and no unlicensed modern English/Korean translation.
- Fictional beings and people remain in the literature layer even when a scenic site gives them statues or rooms.

### 6. What is Liaozhai liqu, and why is it not just soundtrack music?

- Introduce the China ICH classification as traditional music and identify the protecting unit.
- Explain the documented combination of Pu-associated sung texts and circulating tune patterns.
- State carefully that the official ICH record reports fifteen extant works/manuscripts and more than fifty old tune patterns, while also recording losses.
- Add the 2025 scholarly warning: attribution remains a research question; one conference paper challenged works commonly attached to Pu. Present this as a scholarly argument, not a settled correction.
- Memorable discovery 2: textual survival, musical survival, and attribution are three different questions.

### 7. Can a visitor hear Liaozhai liqu today?

- Never infer a daily show from ICH status.
- Give a verification ladder: museum/ICH protecting unit notice → named event organizer → same-week phone/official-account confirmation.
- If no live performance exists, use a rights-cleared institutional recording or displayed score/manuscript; say explicitly that playback is not a live community performance.
- Do not promise a performer, repertoire, venue, or start time without a dated primary notice.

### 8. What does Liuquan tell us—and where does certainty stop?

- Establish Liuquan as an attached protected site through the museum record.
- Present the “serving tea and collecting stories” account only as local/official tourism lore unless a stronger documentary source is secured.
- At the site, read the plaque wording: does it say record, legend, reconstruction, or commemoration?
- Memorable discovery 3: a place may be an authentic memorial node even when its best-known anecdote is not independently proven.

### 9. Is the cemetery worth adding?

- Confirm it as an attached protected site, then make access the deciding condition.
- Useful for readers interested in biography, commemoration, and the author's local rootedness; optional for readers with limited mobility/time.
- No grave photography advice until the current manager's rule is known; use a respectful no-portrait/no-climbing baseline as editorial caution, not a claimed posted regulation.

### 10. What does Liaozhai City document?

- State that the official visitor page describes a later garden/theme complex based on stories.
- Identify fantasy gardens, Liaozhai Palace, light/immersive elements, and “fox spirit” imagery only as operator presentation.
- Use the 2024 government response and project updates to show active redevelopment and partial trial/reopening phases.
- Do not caption modern scenic architecture as Pu's home or a Qing-period set.

### 11. Build a literary half-day from modules, not a rigid clock

Proposed order pending map and access verification:

1. **Core, 90–120 minutes:** current orientation plan → former-residence fabric/restoration labels → museum biography and source objects.
2. **Context, 20–40 minutes:** Liuquan if open and physically connected; classify the plaque's claim.
3. **Commemoration, 30–45 minutes:** cemetery only if officially open, respectful access is clear, and the traveller wants biography.
4. **Reception, 60–90 minutes:** themed attraction only for modern adaptation; skip if the visitor wants manuscripts, architecture, or ICH rather than entertainment.

Exact transfer time must be field-checked; the published plan must fit inside a half-day only after the operator map and access points are verified.

Failure recovery:

- museum closed Monday/temporarily: use current official exterior/heritage interpretation and reschedule; do not substitute the theme park as equivalent evidence;
- Liuquan/cemetery inaccessible: remain in the museum compound and deepen object reading;
- theme park closed: the core literary visit remains complete;
- no live liqu: use an institutional recording/explanation and mark the difference.

### 12. Who should add the themed attraction—and who should skip it?

| Traveller | Recommendation | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| First-time reader, museum-oriented | residence/museum core; skip theme park if time is short | strongest biographical and textual evidence |
| Music/ICH interest | core plus a verified liqu performance/recording | reveals a second Liaozhai corpus |
| Family wanting fantasy entertainment | core first, themed attraction second | keeps entertainment anchored to evidence |
| Looking only for a haunted-house thrill | use the attraction's own current description; this literary page may be skippable | the article will not rank scare effects |
| Mobility/time constrained | museum compound only, after accessibility check | avoids uncertain detached-site transfers |

### 13. Do not mistake these things

- protected former residence ≠ untouched compound;
- *Liaozhai Zhiyi* ≠ a “true ghost file”;
- Liaozhai liqu ≠ incidental theme-park music;
- Liuquan anecdote ≠ automatically verified biography;
- theme-park scene ≠ historical reconstruction unless a specific evidence file says so;
- a 2026 exhibition in Beijing ≠ a guaranteed Zibo gallery object.

### 14. Photography, copyright, and cultural handling

- No film/television still, modern book illustration, attraction show recording, or copyrighted translation without licence.
- A museum object image needs institution permission and object-rights review; “visible in a display case” is not a reuse licence.
- Pair every architecture photo with the exact compound/site identity and restoration status.
- Do not stage a person in costume as Pu, a fox spirit, or a “local ghost” and present it as documentary culture.
- A named Zibo museum/ICH reviewer should check pronunciation, genre terminology, Liuquan wording, and the route's physical feasibility.

### 15. Final verification and close

- Recheck museum closure day/hours, passport or reservation path, detached-site access, theme-park status/tickets, live-performance notice, and photography rules within seven days of publication and again before travel.
- Where primary notices conflict, publish no exact number until the venue resolves it.
- End with a natural option for Homeground to help place the half-day in a Shandong route; no fixed-price product pitch.
