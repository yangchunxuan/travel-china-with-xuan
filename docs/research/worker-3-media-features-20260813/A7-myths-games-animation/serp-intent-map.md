## User-language problems to solve

1. **“Original myth” is often singular.** The future page must replace this with “named earlier version” and explain that literary, religious, oral and visual traditions are layered.
2. **“Based on” is read as “accurate.”** The page should separate borrowed motif, narrative restructuring, new character design and modern theme.
3. **“Real location” is read literally.** A scanned building or texture source is not the fictional place in a novel, film or game.
4. **Modern visual familiarity displaces older evidence.** Searchers often recognize a film/game character design first. Captions must identify the modern design as one copyrighted interpretation.
5. **Spoiler intent conflicts with cultural orientation.** A traveller-facing page should give a spoiler-light reading method; detailed ending explanation belongs elsewhere.

## Recommended future information architecture

If central grants the second authorization, keep one page with this single task:

> Before meeting Wukong or Nezha in a game, film, temple, museum or tourist campaign, identify which layer you are seeing and avoid mistaking an adaptation for a single original myth.

Suggested durable sequence:

1. Direct answer: repetition comes from layered source traditions plus the flexibility of adaptation, not from one immutable plot.
2. A four-layer key: named text, religious/folk practice, performance/visual history, modern work.
3. Wukong worked example: *Journey to the West* as a major literary source, followed by selected modern restructuring.
4. Nezha worked example: older religious/literary layers and visibly different animated constructions.
5. “Fidelity is the wrong score” comparison: retention, combination, omission and invention.
6. Travel encounter: how to read a temple label, museum object or tourism campaign without treating it as franchise scenery.
7. Evidence and rights boundary.
8. Optional B31 box only if every named physical site has direct documentation and current access checking.

## Canonical exclusions

- No plot recap or ending explanation.
- No release/platform/box-office tracker.
- No “all Chinese gods” list.
- No ranking of adaptations by authenticity.
- No separate Wukong/Nezha filming-location or pilgrimage page from this batch.
- No modern stills, screenshots, posters, character sheets or fan art without written rights.

## Recommendation

The search-intent split is manageable, but only through strict scope. Central should authorize the cultural adaptation page, if at all, and explicitly decline product and location-list ownership. The page title can name Wukong and Nezha; its promise must be a method for reading adaptation, not full franchise coverage.
