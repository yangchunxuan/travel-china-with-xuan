## Research conclusion

The topic is viable only after separating four intents:

1. **Cultural adaptation:** why layered literary, religious and performance traditions support repeated retelling. This is the durable A7 opportunity.
2. **Lore/source comparison:** what a current film or game changed from selected earlier texts. Useful as a bounded method, but easily becomes spoiler-heavy and franchise-dependent.
3. **Product information:** release dates, platforms, box office, reviews, sequels and endings. This is news/entertainment intent and should not be the canonical owner.
4. **Location itinerary:** scanned architecture, promoted “inspiration sites,” temples and competing origin claims. This is a separate travel task with high access and attribution risk; B31 should remain a cautious module, not a page.

Recommended canonical task if central authorizes a future article: **help a traveller distinguish inherited motifs, selected textual sources and modern creative choices when encountering Wukong or Nezha in contemporary China.** The article should explain adaptation as a process, not score fidelity.

## Evidence model

| Layer | Appropriate evidence | Safe claim | Unsafe leap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Premodern text | Catalogued edition, scholarly translation or textual study | A motif or episode appears in a named version | This is the single “original Chinese myth” |
| Religious/folk practice | Museum, temple, ethnographic or heritage record tied to place/community | A figure is worshipped or represented in this documented context | All Chinese people share one belief or interpretation |
| Modern adaptation | Official work description and the work itself | The game/film identifies a source or makes a visible creative choice | Marketing language proves historical accuracy |
| Scholarly interpretation | Peer-reviewed adaptation, animation, literature or game study | A named scholar reads a change in a particular way | The interpretation is creator intent or academic consensus |
| Audience question | Search results, forums and reviews | People need source/lore/location distinctions | A popular post proves history, copyright or visitor access |
| Homeground judgment | Transparent comparison of the layers above | A travel-facing reading order or caution | A claim about national identity, ideology or universal reception |

## What can be confirmed now

- *Black Myth: Wukong* is officially presented as inspired by *Journey to the West*; inspiration is not identity with the novel.
- Wukong and Nezha circulate through multiple textual, religious, visual and performance traditions; a modern work selects and changes among them.
- Academic literature treats recent games and animation as adaptations or reconstructions, not transparent windows onto a single fixed myth.
- Current search results mix lore, product, location and cultural questions, making one undifferentiated page unstable.
- Game captures, film stills, posters, trailers and modern character designs should be presumed unavailable for Homeground reuse unless a rights holder grants a suitable written licence.

## What cannot be inferred

- That one Ming novel is the sole origin of every Wukong or Nezha tradition.
- That a modern design is “authentic” because it uses a recognisable weapon, costume or episode.
- That audience popularity proves one interpretation is nationally shared.
- That an architectural site scanned or referenced by a game is the literal setting of the source novel.
- That “official poster,” “press image,” embedded trailer or social-media post means free commercial editorial reuse.
- That an exception in one jurisdiction resolves Homeground’s international publication risk; central must obtain rights or legal review.
