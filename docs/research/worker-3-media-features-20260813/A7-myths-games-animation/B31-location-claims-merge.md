## Central answer

B31 must not become a stand-alone “Wukong and Nezha locations” page in this batch. Its useful material is a compact verification method inside the A7 adaptation research: **a building used as a scan/reference, a place promoted by a tourism campaign, a location associated with an older story, and the fictional setting of a game or animation are four different claims.** One does not prove the next.

The current evidence is asymmetric. A Chinese government source confirms a formal Shanxi tourism campaign linked named heritage places to *Black Myth: Wukong*. No equivalent first-party, production-level source was found that turns online “Nezha inspiration-place” lists into a stable traveller route. Creating one combined itinerary would therefore force unlike evidence into a misleading list.

## Claim types that must remain separate

| Claim | Minimum evidence | What it can establish | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A developer captured or referenced a physical structure | Named developer/production statement, making-of record or institution confirmation identifying the exact structure | The production used that object/place in a stated way | That the in-game building is a documentary reconstruction or the story is set there |
| A government or destination body runs a themed tourism campaign | Dated campaign page naming the work, sites and organizer | The destination officially promoted that route at that time | Developer intent, historical fidelity, permanent access or one-to-one visual identity |
| A heritage place is associated with Wukong, Nezha or an earlier narrative | Site-management, museum, inscription or scholarly evidence tied to that place and tradition | A documented local association in a named context | That a modern game/film copied the site or that it is the character’s single “birthplace” |
| A film/game calls a place its inspiration or prototype | Rights holder, director/designer interview or official production book with precise wording | The production team made that attributed claim | That all visual details came from the place or that visitors can enter production areas |
| A blogger matches screenshots to buildings | Reproducible visual comparison plus a direct source for the exact production relationship | At most, a research lead | Attribution, legal image reuse, current access or literal fictional geography |
| A map or social post labels a “filming location” | First-party production/location record | For live-action work, a documented shoot may have occurred | For animation/game work, the label often confuses reference, scanning, design and setting |

## Black Myth: Wukong — what is currently supportable

1. [Game Science’s official site](https://gamesci.cn/wukong/) confirms the identity of *Black Myth: Wukong*. Its public presentation does not provide Homeground with a reusable image licence or an exhaustive visitor map.
2. [Game Science’s launch release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240820013974/en/Embark-on-a-Journey-to-the-West-Like-Never-Before-in-Black-Myth-Wukong-Available-Today-on-PC-and-PlayStation) supports the work’s stated inspiration from *Journey to the West*. It does not say that every visual setting is a literal place from the novel.
3. [China’s Ministry of Culture and Tourism report on Shanxi’s campaign](https://www.mct.gov.cn/wlbphone/wlbydd/xxfb/qglb/sx/202501/t20250113_957810.html) supports that Shanxi developed a themed cultural-tourism campaign connecting the game with multiple heritage destinations. This is strong evidence that the campaign existed; it is promotional evidence, not a developer-authored catalogue of exact assets.
4. Any named building proposed for a future traveller module needs two additional checks: a direct record of the production relationship and current information from the site’s own management authority. Neither a campaign list nor a screenshot comparison supplies both.

Safe future wording would distinguish verbs: “featured in a tourism campaign,” “identified by the production as reference,” “digitally captured,” “visually compared by fans,” and “associated with an earlier tradition.” These phrases must not collapse into “the game takes place here.”

## Nezha adaptations — why no location list is supportable yet

The reviewed scholarly record concerns how Nezha’s image and narrative change across animation, including [the peer-reviewed 1979–2019 study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-02802-2) and [the study of modern fabulation in *Animation*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17468477221114365). These sources help explain adaptation. They do not nominate a traveller itinerary.

Search results attach Nezha to multiple temples, hometown/origin traditions, production-tourism campaigns and supposed visual prototypes. A local institution may document its own association, but that cannot be generalized into the sole origin of the figure or assumed to be the source for a film design. During this review, no sufficiently precise rights-holder source was verified that names a stable set of film “locations” and explains whether each was a story setting, design reference, promotional partnership or later tourism claim.

Therefore:

- do not publish a Nezha “filming locations” list for animation;
- do not combine competing origin claims into a route without local and scholarly review;
- do not call a temple a film prototype unless the production source uses that precise relationship;
- do not infer a location from architectural resemblance or a character poster;
- retain local sites only as candidates for future item-by-item evidence review.

## Search-result and user-problem finding

Location queries currently mix at least four user needs: finding exact game-reference architecture, recreating viral screenshot angles, visiting places associated with older myths, and following a government promotional route. Results frequently use “取景地” (shooting location), “原型” (prototype), “扫描” (scanned) and “同款” (same as seen) as though they were interchangeable. They are not.

The future A7 box, if authorized, should answer one task only: **before adding a modern-IP place to an itinerary, identify the relationship being claimed and the source that claims it.** It should not try to satisfy screenshot hunting, franchise completism or a nationwide mythology pilgrimage in the same module.

## Traveller verification record for any future named place

Central should require every candidate row to contain:

- exact official Chinese and English place name;
- heritage/management institution and stable place identifier;
- province, city and exact managed component rather than a broad scenic-area label;
- production/work and the precise relationship verb used by a direct source;
- direct production evidence URL and quotation paraphrase;
- site-management URL for current access;
- campaign organizer and campaign date, if applicable;
- ticket/reservation/closure/accessibility check date;
- evidence level: production-confirmed, campaign-confirmed, local-association-only or unresolved comparison;
- image rightsholder, licence and crop restriction;
- explicit “cannot infer” note.

If any of the first six fields is absent, the place should remain research, not itinerary advice.

## Dynamic facts

Checked 2026-08-13. Tourism campaigns, exhibitions, route branding, reservations, opening areas, conservation closures, transport, game updates and production licensing are dynamic. A dated campaign page cannot be promoted into a permanent operating route. Each named property requires a same-date management check before publication and again before a traveller relies on it.

## Image and copyright decision

- No game screenshot, film still, poster, map capture, trailer frame, logo, social-media comparison or fan art is authorized by this note.
- Game Science and film-distributor pages are sources, not reuse licences.
- A traveller’s screenshot does not remove rights in the underlying game or film image.
- A new photograph of a real building can document that building, but its caption cannot claim a production relationship without separate evidence.
- Institutional photographs need item-level reuse terms; government publication does not automatically make them public domain.
- AI recreation is prohibited because it would blur the exact evidentiary relationship the module is meant to clarify.

See A7’s `image-rights-plan.md` for the full asset gate.
