## Direct-answer hypothesis for the first 150 words

Broken Bridge, Leifeng Pagoda and Jinshan Temple are all real places, but none proves that the White Snake story happened as history. Broken Bridge is a long-maintained West Lake landscape feature; beneath the current Leifeng Pagoda is the excavated foundation and crypt of a late-Wuyue Buddhist pagoda, while the tower visitors enter is a twenty-first-century structure; Jinshan is a functioning Buddhist temple complex with its own destruction and rebuilding history, while the flooding of Jinshan belongs to the legend's developing literary and theatrical versions. A traveller who wants the clearest story anchors and strongest material evidence can use one Hangzhou half-day. Add Zhenjiang only when comparing how two cities turn the same legend into landscape is itself the purpose, and when Zhenjiang already fits the route.

This is a drafting hypothesis, not body copy.

## Narrative spine

Move the reader from the wrong question—“Which place is the real story location?”—to the useful question—“Which layer is real here?” The story evolves before the travel route does. Broken Bridge demonstrates that a real, altered landscape feature is not event evidence; Leifeng separates excavated Buddhist remains, the pagoda that collapsed in 1924 and the current interpretive building; Jinshan separates a living temple, changing water-land geography, literary episodes, later place-name associations and current tourism presentation. The conclusion is a route decision, not an origin verdict.

A non-fiction opening image can place three observable elements side by side: the stone bridge at the east end of Bai Causeway, old brick foundations visible below a modern tower, and a hillside temple in Zhenjiang. Do not invent weather, sound, dialogue, a personal visit or a visitor reaction.

## Layer key

- `history`: material fabric, archaeology, dated construction, destruction, repair or documented geography.
- `folklore`: circulating legend, place association, named characters or oral tradition; not verified biography.
- `literature`: identifiable textual, dramatic or screen adaptation and the changes it makes.
- `tourism-presentation`: reconstruction, exhibition, signage, performance, visitor routing or an explicitly labelled Homeground travel judgment.

### Required onsite observation set

Hangzhou:

1. Read Broken Bridge first as the east-end joint of Bai Causeway, then as a story cue; its maintained fabric is not an untouched narrative prop.
2. Use the real distance and sightline between the bridge area and Leifeng on the south shore to break the “compact story walk” illusion.
3. Read the present Leifeng silhouette as modern landscape architecture.
4. Inside, locate the visible old brick base/site within the current protective structure. Treat the excavated underground chamber as archaeological context unless a current official visitor source confirms that it is visible.
5. Treat reliefs, murals and story labels as present-day version selection.

Zhenjiang:

1. Notice the coexistence of the formal temple name `江天禅寺` and the common name `金山寺`.
2. Use stairs and hillside layering to assess mobility before committing to the deep route.
3. Keep Cishou Pagoda separate from Hangzhou's Leifeng Pagoda.
4. If the cave is open, read exactly how `法海洞` and/or `裴公洞` is worded; the naming history is the evidence.
5. View the present water-land relationship only as context for imagination, not proof of a flood event.
6. Give worshippers, monks and religious activity priority over photography.
