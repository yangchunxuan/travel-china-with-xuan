## Stop conditions

- If the restaurant cannot name which items are wild and which are cultivated, do not order the
  wild-mushroom product.
- If it proposes an unexplained mix of wild species, no clear kitchen process, a taste test, raw or
  undercooked service, or drinking alcohol as part of the experience, choose cultivated mushrooms or
  another meal.
- If a late addition is confirmed as the same recorded wild item, staff must start its complete
  process again from that addition. If it is a different or unknown wild mushroom, do not eat from
  the entire pot; restarting a timer is not a recovery.
- If suspicious symptoms occur after the meal, stop eating and seek urgent professional help. Do not
  wait for every diner to develop the same symptoms or try an internet remedy.

## Absolute-language audit

- The guide never calls a named wild species, restaurant, cooking duration or visual sign universally
  safe.
- `Established`, `licensed`, `staff-managed`, `sample retained` and `fully cooked` are layers of risk
  control, not guarantees.
- The one-species first order is explicitly a Homeground editorial response to official no-mixing
  guidance, not an official certification of that species or dish.
