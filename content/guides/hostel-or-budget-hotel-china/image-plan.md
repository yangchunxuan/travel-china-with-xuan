### Why this image

The guide's argument is that the front desk, not the category, decides whether a stay works, and
that the reader should read the notice board in front of them rather than trust a national claim.
This photograph *is* that notice board: a real one, at a real reception desk, legible enough that a
reader can see it lists accepted identity documents and requires real-name, real-time registration,
and signed by one city's public security bureau. The visible attribution to a single city is part of
why it was chosen — it shows the scope limitation the article insists on, inside the image itself.

A staged dormitory or a generic hotel corridor would have decorated the page. This one argues for it.

### An important scope note carried into the body

The board is Longhai City's, and the documents it lists are that city's list. The body says so
explicitly in the callout that references it, and does **not** reproduce the document list as a
national list. Nothing in the guide is sourced to the photograph other than the existence and
general character of such boards.

### Privacy and content checks

- **No people appear in the frame.**
- No personal identity document, bank card, booking screen or order is visible.
- The original frame included a second wall poster carrying staff health-certificate thumbnails and
  a telephone number. **The crop was chosen specifically to exclude it**, so no telephone number and
  no personal document image is present in the delivered file.
- The only text in the delivered image is the public notice board itself and its issuing authority.
