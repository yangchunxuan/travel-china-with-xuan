## The one task this page owns

A foreign traveller already seated at a **Chongqing red-oil hotpot** needs to choose an appropriate
pot, place a controlled first order, recognise the functional menu categories, keep raw and cooked
tools separate, cook without relying on unsafe timing folklore, and recover when the broth is too
spicy or the table has over-ordered.

This is an at-table execution page. Its canonical answer is not a history of hotpot and not a list
of restaurants.

## Original information gain

The page turns six fragments usually scattered across listicles into one reversible workflow:

1. choose the broth before choosing ingredients;
2. order by menu role, not by a long dish list;
3. use half portions or a second round where offered;
4. separate raw and cooked tools and cook small batches thoroughly;
5. treat the oil dip as seasoning/cooling time, not as a safety device;
6. recover from excessive heat, over-ordering, mixed broth or uncertain doneness.

The article also marks where its advice is a Homeground editorial default rather than an official
rule. Official sources define the format and safety boundaries; they do not prescribe the exact
first order suggested here.
