### What the image can explain

The photograph shows a metal grid in one vessel, red broth, active steam, raw meat plates and a
shared table. It supports the article's first decision — understand the vessel and the broth before
ordering a long ingredient list. It also lets the caption warn that a visible grid is not evidence
of sealed broths or individual ownership of a square.

The uploader's caption says the sections had different heat levels. **The article does not repeat
that claim.** A photograph cannot prove how the burner was controlled or establish a general
purpose for every grid.

### Caption boundary

- **Allowed:** “A nine-section hotpot in Chongqing; the dividers are visible, but the image does not
  establish what each section is for.”
- **Not allowed:** “Nine flavours”, “nine diners' private sections”, “three verified heat levels”,
  “traditional since a named dynasty”, or “the authentic Chongqing configuration”.

## Body-image decision

No additional documentary image is published in this draft. The portion matrix, menu-role table and
failure-recovery flow are changeable information and therefore remain HTML, not baked-in graphics.
Adding generic chilli, skyline or restaurant-front photos would not explain the task.

If central later commissions original photography, the useful sequence is:

1. a current menu showing the restaurant's actual broth and half-portion choices, with operator
   permission and prices dated;
2. raw and cooked tongs shown separately, staged with staff approval and no hygiene interference;
3. a plain oil-and-garlic dip beside one cooked ingredient, without claiming it neutralises heat;
4. the bill screen with all personal, table, order and payment identifiers removed.

Each future photograph needs venue, date, photographer, written commercial-use permission, visible
person consent, exact menu identity, and separate EN/ZH/KO alt and caption review. No AI-generated
documentary image is acceptable.
