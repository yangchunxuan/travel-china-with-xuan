### Why this image and not a dune, a camel or a cave

The guide's argument is that Dunhuang is the far end of a long corridor and must be treated as a
base rather than a stop. This frame carries exactly that: a real, specific station with its name
board visible, a very large empty forecourt, low desert light and almost nobody in it. A dune
photograph would illustrate a mood; the Mogao cliff belongs to the Mogao canonical owner and would
blur the boundary this page is careful to keep. The station is the thing the reader will actually
arrive at, and its emptiness is the point.

### Age of the photograph

The photograph dates from 2013. It is used as an establishing image of the station **building**,
which is a fixed structure, and the body makes no claim that depends on the photograph's date. No
operational fact — service, signage, transfer arrangement — is drawn from the image. Flagged here so
central can weigh whether a newer frame should be sought later; nothing in the current text is
falsified by the age.

### Privacy and content checks

- No identity documents, bank cards, tickets, order screens or telephone numbers are legible.
- Two people are visible, both small, distant and incidental in a large public forecourt. Neither
  is identifiable at the delivered resolution.
- The only legible text is the station name board and street furniture.
