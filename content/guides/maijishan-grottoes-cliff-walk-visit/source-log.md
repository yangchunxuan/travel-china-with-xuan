## 1. Dunhuang Academy — Maijishan Grottoes visitor page: **VERIFIED, read in full**

- **Institution:** 敦煌研究院 (Dunhuang Academy), which administers Maijishan as one of the six
  sites under its "one institute, six locations" structure
- **Page title:** 麦积山石窟, within 石窟行旅
- **URL:** https://www.dha.ac.cn/skxl/mjssk.htm
- **Published / updated:** undated live page; the same site's announcement index carries items dated
  to August 2026, so the site is current
- **Retrieved:** 2026-08-22 · **Rechecked:** 2026-08-23
- **Supports:**
  - Open all year. **Peak season 1 April – 31 October, 08:30–17:30. Off season 1 November –
    31 March, 09:00–17:00.**
  - Location: 甘肃省天水市麦积区泉湖路2号.
  - Official booking is via the official ticketing account, with ticket detail on the Maijishan
    Grottoes ticketing site.
  - **Two ticket types, designated A and B, at different prices**, each with a half-price tier.
  - Guided-commentary tiers and their charges, and a ticketing enquiry telephone number.
  - **Free-admission categories are processed in person at a designated window at the 峡门
    visitor service centre, within a stated daily window, valid for that day only.** Half-price
    categories are listed separately.
  - **The visitor rules quoted throughout the body**, in the operator's own words: visitors must
    assess their own physical condition before entering the cave area and **those with high blood
    pressure or low blood sugar should be cautious about entering the walkways**; no smoking or
    pets; **in the core cave area all hanging items — water bottles, keys, phones — must be put in a
    bag or left at storage, to prevent objects falling from height and injuring visitors**; no
    camera or phone flash and no touching, with violation ending the visit immediately; **the
    walkways are narrow, so queue and enter in order, keep a steady pace, do not photograph on the
    walkways and avoid lingering**; **rain or snow causes minor rockfall, and at the west cliff exit
    visitors should keep to the inner side and pass quickly**; **two toilets only, at the west cliff
    exit and below the plaza steps, with none on the walkways**.
  - Public transport routes from Tianshui's stations and airport are published here. **None was
    reproduced in the body**, by design.
- **Cannot support:** which caves are open on a date; capacity or quota; the walkway's condition on
  a given day; anything about Tianshui as a city.
- **Dynamic:** yes for hours, ticket types, prices and the concession window.

## 2. Dunhuang Academy — Maijishan conservation feature: **VERIFIED, read in full**

- **Institution:** 敦煌研究院, quoting **麦积山石窟艺术研究所** (Maijishan Grottoes Art Research
  Institute) deputy director 岳永强 at length, and **甘肃省文物局** (Gansu Provincial Cultural
  Heritage Bureau) director 程亮
- **Page title:** 潜心护宝 让"东方微笑"绽放新光彩——甘肃天水麦积山石窟文物保护谱写新篇章
- **URL:** https://www.dha.ac.cn/info/1018/5848.htm
- **Published:** 2024-09-20 · **Retrieved:** 2026-08-22 · **Rechecked:** 2026-08-23
- **Supports:**
  - **221 surviving caves, 10,632 statues of various kinds, more than 1,000 m² of wall painting**;
    the epithet 东方雕塑陈列馆.
  - **1961: listed by the State Council in the first batch of national key cultural relic protection
    units.** **2014: inscribed on the World Heritage List as one of the 33 component sites of the
    Silk Roads: the Routes Network of Chang'an–Tianshan Corridor.**
  - The hill is **142 m high at an elevation of 1,671.4 m**, southeast of Tianshui's Maiji District,
    named for its resemblance to a stack of harvested wheat.
  - Carving chronology: began in the Later Qin of the Sixteen Kingdoms; peaked under the Northern
    Wei; continued under the Western Wei; the Northern Zhou **Seven Buddha Niche** was the largest
    undertaking; by the Sui **the cliff face was already covered with caves and walkways**.
  - **In the Ming and Qing, part of the west cliff walkway collapsed and the route was severed;
    with no one to repair it, some caves were not entered again after the 17th century.**
  - **September 1953:** the Maijishan Grottoes cultural relics management institute was established.
  - Reinforcement history: survey from 1960; 1964 proposals; **1973 approval** of "reinforce the
    east cliff, relocate the west cliff"; **1976 adoption of the shotcrete-and-anchor scheme**;
    described as **the largest and longest grotto-reinforcement project in China's history**, and
    awarded a **national science and technology progress third prize in 1985**.
  - **The reinforcement project replaced the original timber walkways with reinforced concrete
    walkways.**
  - **Walkway construction:** cantilever beams, inclined beams, deck slabs, steps and railings;
    cantilever beams **anchored generally 4 m into the cliff**, **exposed portion generally 2–4 m**,
    **spaced 3 m**; **total design safety factor not less than 2.5**; **design live load
    350 kg/m²**.
  - Planned **dedicated walkway monitoring system** covering visitor numbers, component deformation,
    stress and strain, and vibration, with **visitor numbers strictly controlled by section and at
    key nodes according to walkway load requirements**; a stated 50- or 100-year service-life
    horizon implying stricter maintenance, inspection and visitor management.
  - The deputy director's statement that **the walkway is itself a constituent part of the grotto
    complex with cultural-relic value and attributes**.
  - The quoted remark that **the path is very well maintained, but you still need to assess your own
    stamina** — used in the body as the framing for self-assessment.
  - Seepage: the rainy, humid climate is a conservation difficulty; a working principle of
    **sealing inside the caves and draining across the cave area**, with an explicit acknowledgement
    that seepage control cannot be resolved in one go.
- **Cannot support:** current opening arrangements, ticketing, or the state of any section today.
- **Dynamic:** low for history and engineering; the monitoring system is described as planned.
