### Why this image

The article asks the reader to make one judgement before booking: can I do the walkways? This frame
gives them what they need to start answering it. The cliff is seen from below, from roughly where a
visitor first sees it, so the height reads honestly; the tiers of walkway are visible as horizontal
lines across the rock; the cave openings are visible above them; and the temple roofs and forecourt
at the foot give the scale.

It also carries the article's secondary point without a caption: **looking up at this face from the
ground is itself the visit for anyone who decides the walkways are not for them.** A close
photograph of sculpture inside a cave would have been prettier and would have argued for the wrong
thing.

### Licence and provenance checks performed

- Licence read from the Commons file page's own `extmetadata`, not inferred from a search thumbnail
  or a reverse-image result.
- CC0 is a public domain dedication, so attribution is not legally required. It is given anyway, in
  the `heroCredit` block of all three locales, in each language's sources block, and here.
- The image is a photograph. **No AI-generated image is used anywhere in this guide**, and none
  would be acceptable for a documentary depiction of a protected heritage site.
- No interior or mural photography is used anywhere in this guide, which is consistent with the
  site's own rule against photographing the grottoes with flash and with the general sensitivity of
  cave imagery.

### Privacy and content checks

- **No people appear in the frame.**
- No identity documents, bank cards, tickets, order screens or telephone numbers.
- Some Chinese signage is visible on the temple gate and the forecourt notice board, but at the
  delivered resolution none of it carries readable personal or commercial information.

### Crop review

- **Desktop (wide):** the cliff occupies the upper left and the temple gate the lower right, so the
  vertical scale reads immediately; the sky at top left carries the headline overlay cleanly.
- **Mobile (390 px):** the shared template renders the same 1.6:1 ratio. The cliff face and its
  walkway tiers remain in frame and clear of the outer 8% that a narrow viewport can clip.

### Age of the photograph

Taken November 2017. It depicts the **landform and the walkway system**, both of which are stable
and, in the walkway's case, explicitly maintained as a protected structure. No operational claim in
the body is drawn from the image, and the body sources hours, tickets and rules separately.

### Not used

No second image, and no Homeground-created diagram. A simple elevation showing the approach, the
climb and the walkway tiers would help readers judge the exposure, and is recommended to central as
a future addition; it could not be produced here without committing a file beyond the nine allowed.
