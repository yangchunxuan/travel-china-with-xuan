## Authority and conflict reconciliation

The current national licensing rule controls the duration framework; MPS Order No. 90 supplies the still-published rental-vehicle venue and vehicle-category rules. NIA and the State Council portal provide visitor guidance; NIA expressly labels its page for reference. Local traffic-management authorities own the operational checklist and issue decision. Rental providers own the separate handover contract.

NIA's reference page generally points to an office where the visitor enters or resides, while Article 11 directs a driver of a rented Chinese vehicle to the authority where the rental unit is located. This is disclosed rather than harmonised. The traveller must ask traffic management in the intended rental location to confirm the handling venue in writing. The article does not substitute the separate rule for a self-imported vehicle.

Beijing currently describes an in-person application with two photos and specified translation acceptance. Its precise traffic-management page states an 18+ floor for small cars, an ability test at 70+, and exemption from submitting a body-condition certificate for temporarily entering small-car/motorcycle applicants. Shanghai describes a local photo/application process, road-law learning, an online route and its current age conditions. These local facts are not merged into a universal checklist. If Chinese rules and English guidance differ, the guide records the versions and asks the competent office rather than choosing the easier text.

## Demand research record

Google was sampled on 2026-09-01 for queries around `can tourists drive in China`, `China temporary driving permit`, `international driving permit China` and `rent car China foreign licence`. Results repeatedly mixed foreign licences, IDPs, Chinese temporary permits and rental-company requirements. Visible questions showed the practical gap was not a car-rental ranking but the two-gate decision: legal permission first, commercial handover second.
