## Selection rationale and comparison requirement

The selected real, accurately located documentary photograph shows a named urban-wall section in relation to its current road and surrounding terrain. It does not provide a component-level conservation record, so neither the alt text nor caption claims that visible masonry is original or repaired.

Any future comparison photograph must separately record:

- exact city, wall section, gate or management unit;
- capture date and photographer;
- source page, licence or written permission, required credit and crop permission;
- the component-level heritage or conservation record used by the caption;
- original-file SHA-256, published-derivative SHA-256 and all crop/resize operations.

The selected 1600 × 1000 derivative preserves the wall–approach relationship rather than isolating picturesque brick texture.

The final EN, ZH and KO alt text above identifies only what the image and source record jointly establish. It does not say that repaired masonry is visible.

| Evidence class | Exact target | Caption boundary | Rights requirement |

| Surviving fabric | A named Nanjing section or one of the Beijing heritage authority's identified remnants | State the exact section and what the authority identifies as surviving; do not date every brick | Compatible open licence or photographer permission, plus current site rules |

| Archaeological trace | A managed exposed wall line, gate foundation or road alignment | Distinguish exposed remains, marked outline and reconstructed display | Archaeological authority context and photographer permission |
| Urban form | A view through a named gate toward a documented street or axis | Separate historical-route evidence from present-day streetscape observation | Local reporting preferred; protect identifiable people |

## Exclusions

- No generic “ancient Chinese wall” image without an exact place.
- No Great Wall photograph; this canonical concerns urban walls and gates.
- No AI-generated or AI-assisted documentary wall, gate, reconstruction or excavation.
- No claim of “original,” “Ming brick,” “restored section” or “ritual axis” inferred from appearance alone.
- No tourism-board, museum, news, social-media or official-site image unless reuse and crop rights are explicit.
- No photograph of a complete modern circuit captioned as continuously surviving historic fabric.
