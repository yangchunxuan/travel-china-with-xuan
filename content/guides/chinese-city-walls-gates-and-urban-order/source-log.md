## Core evidence

1. [City Walls of the Ming and Qing Dynasties](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/5324/), UNESCO World Heritage Centre: a State Party Tentative List dossier, not a World Heritage inscription. It supports the submitted comparative claims and the Nanjing/Xi'an component histories only with that attribution.
2. [Nanjing City Wall protection ordinance](https://www.nanjing.gov.cn/zdgk/201605/t20160506_1056849.html), Nanjing Municipal Government: legal protection scope and management principles; it cannot date every visible brick.
3. [2025 conservation of the Jiefangmen–Xuanwumen section](https://www.nanjing.gov.cn/njxx/202508/t20250826_5635731.html), Nanjing Municipal Government: cracking, weathering, water damage, structural work and minimum intervention at the named section; it does not identify every repair interface elsewhere.
4. [Xi'an City Wall protection ordinance](https://wwj.shaanxi.gov.cn/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/flfg/fg/201704/t20170403_2131136.html), Shaanxi Provincial Cultural Heritage Administration: protected object includes the Ming wall body, gates, ancillary buildings, moat and sites/remains; it does not make all standing components Ming originals.
5. [Xi'an City Wall repairs and preventive monitoring](https://en.xa.gov.cn/MediaCenter/News/1738099529058168833.html), Xi'an Municipal Government: major repair programme from 1983 and later monitoring; used as a first-party account, not a material-by-material survey.
6. [Surviving Ming Beijing city-wall components](https://wwj.beijing.gov.cn/bjww/362771/362779/dqpqgzdwwbhdw/523514/index.html), Beijing Municipal Cultural Heritage Bureau: identifies the Dongbianmen and Xibianmen sections and the Zuo'anmen guardroom as surviving components.
7. [Reconstructing Yongdingmen](https://www.unesco.org/zh/articles/zhongguofujianyongdingmenyushijieyichanjiazhidechanshi), UNESCO expert article: the tower was demolished in 1957 and reconstructed on its former site in 2004 using survey drawings and photographs. The current superstructure is not described as surviving 1553 fabric.
8. [Datong Ancient City](https://www.dt.gov.cn/dtszf/stdt/201811/cbe3380bd8014f1cb4979371687dee29.shtml), Datong Municipal Government: current page dated 2026-03-25 describing the project launched in 2008, repaired or recreated structures and the four sides joined in 2016. Its promotional language is not used to claim continuous Ming material.
9. [Social-mobility controls in Tang Chang'an](https://www.nopss.gov.cn/n1/2019/1216/c219470-31507572.html), National Office for Philosophy and Social Sciences: an essay by a Shaanxi Normal University history professor documenting gate opening and closing, curfew, patrols and gate guards in one capital and period; not a universal Chinese system.
10. [Archaeological research on Han Chang'an](https://www.archaeology.sdu.edu.cn/info/1027/2884.htm), Shandong University School of Archaeology: supports recovering urban form from wall lines, gates and roads even when a complete wall does not stand.

## Editorial corrections and evidence levels

- The UNESCO wall dossier is labelled a Tentative List submission rather than a World Heritage inscription.
- Yongdingmen is explicitly labelled a documented 2004 reconstruction after the 1957 demolition; the present upper structure is not called surviving Ming material.
- Datong is described through the municipal record's repair/recreation and 2016 closure of the circuit. A uniform appearance is not treated as proof of continuous historic fabric.
- Tang Chang'an's gate-control system remains tied to its named city and period.
- Material dates are never inferred from colour, weathering, brick neatness or a traditional silhouette.
- Government and heritage sources support protected components and dated interventions. Named scholarship supports the historical interpretation. The five-label vocabulary, walking sequence and recovery steps are Homeground editorial tools.
