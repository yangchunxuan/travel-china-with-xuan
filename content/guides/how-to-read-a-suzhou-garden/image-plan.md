# Image plan — how-to-read-a-suzhou-garden

Status: COPY DRAFT — EDITORIAL REVIEW AND ASSETS NEEDED

## 1. Hero

Use one real, licensed documentary photograph made inside a named Suzhou classical garden. The ideal frame is taken from a covered corridor, doorway or lattice opening and contains at least three readable layers: architectural foreground, water or rock in the middle ground, and a pavilion, tree or borrowed element beyond. Lingering Garden or Master-of-Nets Garden would demonstrate the article's spatial argument well, but the caption must name the actual garden and viewpoint. Avoid a drone view that reveals the complete plan at once. Target: 1600 × 1000 WebP, with photographer, capture date, exact garden, viewpoint and usage-rights record.

## 2. Sequential-view pair

If a supporting set is available, use two real photographs made a few steps apart along the same route: one before a corridor turn or threshold and one after it. The pair should demonstrate concealment and release without pretending to be a universal route. Keep lens choice and editing honest; do not use a compressed telephoto image to manufacture a borrowed view that is not apparent to a visitor.

## 3. Rock, water and window detail

One optional documentary close-up can explain either a limestone rockery as navigable terrain, a reflection that joins architecture and water, or a lattice opening that frames an internal scene. Identify the garden and feature through the operator, conservation centre or an experienced editor. Do not label every framed opening as borrowed scenery; an internal framed view may be `框景` rather than `借景`.

## 4. Official plans, maps and museum material

An official plan of one garden or a Suzhou Garden Museum interpretive diagram could help explain route sequence, but only if reuse permission and required credit are documented. A screenshot of the UNESCO component map has limited explanatory value and carries its own terms of use; link the live record instead unless the editor secures permission. A photograph of The Met's Astor Court may be useful for the Master-of-Nets connection, but The Met's object-image and brand terms must be checked separately.

## 5. Rights, branding and visitor privacy

UNESCO, Suzhou garden authorities, museums and photographers may each impose different image licences. A publicly viewable page is not automatic permission to republish its photograph. Preserve required credits, do not crop out institutional marks contrary to licence terms, and avoid implying endorsement by UNESCO or a garden operator. Obtain permission for identifiable visitors where required. Do not use AI-generated gardens, historical owners, craftsmen, visitors or “documentary” Suzhou scenes.

## 6. Keep the explanatory system in HTML

The six-element garden grammar, framed-view versus borrowed-scenery comparison, four-garden choice table, seven-stop walking exercise and evidence-level table should remain accessible HTML. These concepts will need editorial correction and should not be frozen into infographics. The article does not need decorative diagrams simply to increase image count.
