## Search gap

The dominant gap is not a missing plot summary. It is the absence of a compact, source-led explanation that lets a visitor hold four true but different things at once:

- Shilin is a multi-stage karst landscape.
- Ashima belongs to a living Sani Yi oral tradition with variants.
- twentieth-century editing and the 1964 film made influential adaptation choices;
- a natural pillar became a named cultural landmark through viewpoint and interpretation.

Many overview and travel results collapse these layers into “the legend says she became the rock,” leave the film's influence unattributed, or imply that every Shilin landmark shares the same UNESCO status. Official and academic sources contain the corrective evidence but do not combine it into a practical onsite reading sequence.

## Information gain

The page earns a distinct result by contributing original editorial structure rather than another encyclopedic summary:

1. An answer-first statement separates physical cause from cultural recognition.
2. A four-layer table tells readers what geology, oral tradition, adaptation and tourism presentation can and cannot prove.
3. A material–structure–water explanation turns official geology into observable joints, bedding, grooves, fissures and gaps.
4. A sourced adaptation comparison assigns the sibling-to-romance change to scholarship and uses the film archive only for the film facts it actually supports.
5. A two-angle method explains why a natural silhouette is recognisable without inventing a marked viewpoint.
6. A World Heritage warning prevents designation inflation.
7. A field sequence and recovery table remain useful without a museum, show or formation panel.
8. Seven individually reviewed real images show the rock, its wider landscape, designation, language, manuscript presentation and material-culture context without AI documentary substitution.
