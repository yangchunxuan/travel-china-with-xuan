# Canonical boundary — medical-emergency-in-china-for-travellers

Reviewed: **2026-09-01**
Freshness class: **critical-on-emergency-number-or-dispatch-protocol-change**

## Sole canonical ownership

The immediate task path for a foreign traveller facing a medical emergency in mainland China: call the national pre-hospital emergency number 120 without waiting for translation, payment or insurance confirmation; give a usable location and concise condition report; maintain the dispatcher connection; prepare safe access and a compact handoff; then preserve hospital, payment and insurance records without promising an outcome.

This page merges these intents into one owner:

- ambulance and medical-emergency number in mainland China
- what a traveller should say to 120
- how to prepare location and patient information for dispatch
- what to do when language, location, phone contact or ambulance access fails
- what to carry and record during ambulance-to-hospital handoff

## Emergency override

Immediate danger comes before article completion, translation setup, document collection, payment, insurance, photography or contacting Homeground. Call 120 and follow the dispatcher and on-scene professionals. Only take an action yourself when it does not expose you or the patient to additional danger and is directed by a competent professional or covered by training you genuinely have.

## Official conflict rule

The national NHC rule establishes 120 as the nationwide pre-hospital medical-emergency number. An NHC response records that Beijing also has 999; older visitor material may list it without a jurisdiction warning. The article does not turn 999 into a national backup or route callers away from 120. If local official instructions differ, preserve source, place and date, ask the local health authority to clarify for future use, and do not delay an active 120 call.
