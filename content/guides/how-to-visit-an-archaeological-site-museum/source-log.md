## Canonical and editorial boundary

Existing Liangzhu, Sanxingdui, Terracotta Warriors and Nanhai No. 1 guides retain their venue-specific visit, booking, route or collection tasks. This page owns only the generic method.

The copy keeps three evidence layers separate:

1. an observed or recorded fact, such as a labelled layer, findspot, material or conservation condition;
2. an institutional or scholarly interpretation, such as a probable function, date range or reconstruction;
3. an unresolved question, where the available evidence does not support one final answer.

`Archaeological site museum`, `on-site museum`, `in-situ museum` and `archaeological park` are not treated as universal synonyms. Nor does the page claim that every site museum sits above an excavation, that every site park has an indoor museum, or that a reconstruction is in situ.

## Official and institutional sources reviewed

1. **National Archaeological Site Park Management Measures**, National Cultural Heritage Administration text republished by Chengde Cultural Relics Bureau
   - https://wwj.chengde.gov.cn/art/2022/7/26/art_2877_866682.html
   - Supports the national archaeological site park definition and the linked protection, archaeology, research, interpretation, public service and monitoring functions.
   - Boundary: the national designation is not applied to every site museum in China.

2. **2024 National Archaeological Site Park operation-assessment notice**, National Cultural Heritage Administration notice mirrored by Shaanxi Provincial Cultural Heritage Administration
   - https://wwj.shaanxi.gov.cn/sy/dtyw/tzgg/qtgg/202410/t20241011_3017277.html
   - Supports the coexistence of routine public opening, a site museum or display centre, continuing archaeology, monitoring and educational activity.
   - Boundary: assessment criteria are not a promise that visitors can see active fieldwork.

3. **Cultural Relics Protection Law of the People's Republic of China**, adopted 2024 and effective 2025-03-01, National People's Congress
   - https://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202411/t20241108_440882.html
   - Supports value interpretation by museums and archaeological site parks and the controlled, approved nature of archaeological excavation.
   - Boundary: public access never authorises entry into an excavation, conservation workspace or closed zone.

4. **Relics Hall**, Jinsha Site Museum
   - https://www.jinshasitemuseum.com/site-75/node/315
   - Supports the named Jinsha example: a protective hall over excavated remains, and the documented backfilling of an elephant-tusk pit when effective in-situ protection was unavailable.
   - Boundary: Jinsha's design and conservation decisions are not generalised to every Chinese site.

5. **Museum composition**, Jinsha Site Museum
   - https://www.jinshasitemuseum.com/site-75/node/287
   - Supports the distinction among the Relics Hall, Exhibition Hall, conservation/restoration centre and wider site environment.

6. **Xi'an Banpo Museum**
   - https://www.banpomuseum.com.cn/
   - Used only as another institutional model of a museum connected to a prehistoric settlement site; no dynamic visitor information is copied.

7. **Archaeological site museums bibliography**, International Council of Museums
   - https://icom.museum/en/ressource/musees-de-site-archeologique/
   - Used for professional terminology and in-situ conservation context.

## Independent cross-checks

1. Bai, Lu and Zhou, Shuang-Lin, **Issues of In Situ Conservation at Jinsha, People's Republic of China**, *Conservation and Management of Archaeological Sites* 14.
   - https://doi.org/10.1179/1350503312Z.00000000022
   - Supports the point that a protective structure must balance environmental control, public interpretation and continuing site management. It is one case study, not a national operating rule.

   - https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13505033.2024.2414526
   - Supports the caution that site-museum and archaeological-park terminology overlaps across institutions and jurisdictions.

## Dynamic and safety recheck

- The article deliberately contains no venue opening hours, admission price, reservation interface, transport timetable or promise that a named gallery or object is available.
- Before a real visit, the traveller must use the venue's current official notice to confirm which site, exhibition and visitor-route components are open.
- Closures, backfilling, covered remains, controlled light, barriers and visible supports may reflect preservation, safety or continuing research; the reason should be taken from current official interpretation rather than guessed.
- Never cross a barrier, touch remains, enter a work zone, or use a social-media location pin as permission to reach a closed archaeological area.
