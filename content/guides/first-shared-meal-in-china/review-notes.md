## Required review scope

The reviewer should check the cultural meaning and ordinary spoken tone in all three locales, not only grammar.

1. Confirm that the casual-restaurant scenario is recognisable without being presented as a national rule.
2. Check the distinction between an ordinary meal, a family home meal, a formal banquet and a business dinner.
3. Verify that seating guidance does not turn formal honour-seat diagrams into an everyday requirement.
4. Verify the descriptions of who leads ordering, when people begin eating, serving food to another person's bowl and the use of public chopsticks or serving spoons.
5. Check that tea guidance remains general and does not duplicate Guangzhou morning-tea customs, tea-seat fees, dim sum ordering or finger-tapping stories.
6. Check the payment distinction among a clear invitation, peer splitting, taking turns and an ambiguous meal.
7. Confirm that the article does not advise deliberate food waste and that packing leftovers is described naturally.
8. Check the Mandarin phrases and their English and Korean explanations for real conversational use.

## Explicitly excluded claims

- “The oldest person always starts.”
- “The host always orders and always pays.”
- “Chinese friends never split a bill.”
- “Every restaurant uses a round table, Lazy Susan or QR menu.”
- “Tea is always free” or “tea is always charged.”
- “Guests should deliberately leave food to prove the host was generous.”
- Any rule presented as applying to every region, generation, family or meal.
