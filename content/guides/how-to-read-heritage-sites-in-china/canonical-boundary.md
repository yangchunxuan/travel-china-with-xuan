# Canonical boundary — How to Read Heritage Sites in China

Reviewed: 2026-08-15

## This page owns

- A **China-wide visitor method** for separating material age, location, intervention and evidence source.
- Seven working categories: surviving historic fabric, conserved in place, conservation repair, restoration, reconstruction, relocation and modern historicizing construction.
- Correct use of the UNESCO Nara document and current World Heritage authenticity guidance without reducing authenticity to old material.
- Three bounded cases that demonstrate different evidence problems:
  - **Yinxu:** in-situ remains, archaeological layers, protective backfill and museum interpretation.
  - **Jiankou Great Wall:** archaeology-repair linkage, emergency stabilization, minimum intervention, traditional craft and material records.
  - **Yongdingmen:** historical loss, modern reconstruction and later conservation of that reconstruction.
- A traveller workflow for reading labels, recording uncertainty and recovering when remains are invisible, intervention language is vague or a project is under construction.

## Existing wall guide retains ownership

The published guide `chinese-city-walls-gates-and-urban-order` remains the canonical owner for:

- How walls, gates, towers, barbicans, moats, streets and city axes function as an urban and defensive system.
- How to read a gate sequence and the relationship between fortification and street order.
- Its existing city-wall examples, including Nanjing and Xi'an.

This new page does **not** reproduce the Nanjing or Xi'an passages, create a second national city-wall primer, or teach fortification terminology. It links to the existing wall guide only after explaining how to identify material and intervention status.

## Pages this draft must not spawn

- Separate thin pages for “Is Yinxu original?”, “Was Jiankou rebuilt?” or “Is Yongdingmen fake?”
- A city-by-city authenticity verdict list
- A generic “Chinese restoration philosophy” page without component-level evidence
- A replica/theme-park list
- A legal guide that turns the seven editorial labels into purported statutory categories

## Search and editorial boundary

- Primary intent: understand how to inspect and describe a heritage intervention.
- It is not a current-access guide, engineering manual, legal opinion or authenticity scorecard.
- A site or component can carry several values and intervention histories; the page does not issue a single “real/fake” rating.
- Dynamic access and construction facts stay dated and secondary to the durable method.
