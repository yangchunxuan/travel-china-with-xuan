## Document identity correction

The Nara source used in the article is the UNESCO World Heritage Committee information document:

- **Title:** *The Nara Document on Authenticity*
- **Identifier:** `WHC-94/CONF.003/INF.008`
- **Document date:** 21 November 1994
- **Committee context:** eighteenth session of the World Heritage Committee
- **Expert meeting reported:** Nara, Japan, 1–6 November 1994
- **Archive URL:** <https://whc.unesco.org/archive/nara94.htm>

UNESCO catalogue record 116018 is a real record for the Nara document, but the downloadable record has legacy fields that can be misread. It is retained as a cross-check, not used to override the committee document's identifier, meeting year or date. The article does not present the Nara document as legislation or as a permission to declare any intervention authentic.

## Evidence boundaries retained in all three languages

- **Original material is not treated as the only possible authenticity attribute.** Material age remains one distinct question.
- **Repair is not treated as falsification.** The draft asks what was stabilized, retained, added and documented.
- **World Heritage inscription is not used as a certificate for every visible component.** Property, component and material scales stay separate.
- **Yinxu surface treatments are not called Shang fabric.** A vegetation outline may communicate a protected plan while the archaeological evidence remains below.
- **The Jiankou image is not a repair record.** It predates phase five and supplies only verified place context.
- **The Yongdingmen image is not called an ancient tower.** It depicts the modern reconstruction before the 2026 repair.
- **No visitor-access promise is made for Jiankou or Yongdingmen.** Current works and ordinary visitor access are separate dynamic facts.
- **No appearance-only dating is permitted.** Weathering, colour and traditional style do not independently establish age.

## Language and terminology notes

- EN uses “surviving historic fabric,” “conserved in place,” “conservation repair,” “restoration,” “reconstruction,” “relocation,” and “modern historicizing construction.”
- ZH deliberately uses the seven task terms: `原构 / 原址保留 / 保护性修缮 / 复原 / 重建 / 迁建 / 现代仿古`.
- KO uses functional equivalents and retains the Chinese form at first occurrence where it prevents category drift: `현존 역사 재료·원형 부재 / 원위치 보존 / 보존 보수 / 복원 / 재건 / 이건 / 전통 양식 모방 신축`.
- “Yin Xu” follows the UNESCO English property name in sources; the running English text uses “Yinxu.” Chinese uses “殷墟”; Korean first occurrence uses “인쉬(殷墟).”
- “Jiankou Great Wall” is “箭扣长城” and “젠커우 장성(箭扣长城).”
- “Yongdingmen” is “永定门” and “융딩먼.”
