# Image plan and rights record — How to Read Heritage Sites in China

Status: **five real, place-specific files prepared; central editorial and licence notice review required before publication**

- Reviewed: 2026-08-14
- Documentary policy: no AI-generated documentary image, no film/television still, no generic “old-looking” architecture and no image-location guessing
- Processing: ordinary crop, resize and WebP conversion only; no generative fill or semantic alteration; output files were visually inspected after conversion and EXIF was stripped
- Rights model: each derivative retains the source licence and attribution; ShareAlike derivatives must continue under the corresponding CC BY-SA version
- Shared provenance registry: intentionally not modified on this isolated article branch; all provenance is recorded below for central transfer

## Deliberate image gaps and central options

1. **No current Jiankou phase-five engineering photograph is reused.** The official project pages are evidence sources, but their page images were not assumed to carry a commercial-compatible reuse licence. A later image must identify the phase, tower or wall section, date, photographer and permission.
2. **No historical Yongdingmen photograph is included.** A location-specific archival image with a clearly reusable, commercial-compatible licence was not closed. The text and caption therefore establish loss/reconstruction through records, not visual comparison.
3. **No generic ancient building fills either gap.** Apparent age is the exact inference this article warns against.
4. **Tables stay as HTML.** The seven-label comparison, scale distinction and recovery matrix must remain editable and dated rather than being rendered as hard-to-update graphics.
