- Reviewed: 2026-08-15 (Asia/Shanghai)
- Canonical owner: `/guides/how-to-read-heritage-sites-in-china/`
- Page job: help a traveller distinguish surviving fabric, in-situ protection, conservation repair, reconstruction, relocation and modern interpretation before making a claim about a Chinese heritage site.
- Do not create: city variants, one page per intervention label, “real versus fake China” lists, authenticity rankings, or duplicate pages for restored/rebuilt/reconstructed wording.

## Primary query

`are heritage sites in China original or rebuilt`

The search task is broader than a single monument. The page should answer it with a repeatable field method, then use Yinxu, Jiankou and Yongdingmen as bounded examples rather than turning those cases into a national verdict.

## Search task

The reader wants a reliable way to interpret what is visible without mistaking visual age for material history. The page satisfies `understand` first and then supplies an on-site evidence workflow. It does not decide whether an entire country, property or reconstruction is “authentic.”

## SERP composition observed on 2026-08-15

The sampled English, Chinese and Korean results were dominated by the Nara Document, conservation principles, academic debates, individual reconstruction controversies and general traveller discussions. Few results translated those materials into a neutral field method that separates object, component, property and setting.

Representative results:

- [UNESCO World Heritage Centre — Nara Document archive](https://whc.unesco.org/archive/nara94.htm) — primary international record for the context-dependent authenticity framework.
- [ICOMOS — Principles for the Conservation of Heritage Sites in China](https://openarchive.icomos.org/id/eprint/1650/) — professional conservation principles and terminology.
- [Beijing Municipal Commission of Planning and Natural Resources — heritage lecture](https://ghzrzyw.beijing.gov.cn/zhengwuxinxi/zxzt/mcbhdjt/yjnr/202105/t20210501_2380482.html) — official Chinese discussion of authenticity and conservation practice.
- Traveller forum discussions asking whether visible structures are original or rebuilt — useful only as demand discovery, not as factual authority.

This is a dated qualitative observation, not a ranking guarantee, search-volume claim or traffic estimate.

## Result gap and Homeground information gain

- Give the reader seven bounded labels instead of a binary “real or fake” verdict.
- Separate material, location, form, function, tradition, setting and evidence source rather than treating authenticity as one visual property.
- Make scale explicit: an altered component does not automatically settle the status of the whole property, and World Heritage inscription does not certify every visible element as ancient.
- Use three cases documented in official sources with different evidence chains: archaeological presentation at Yinxu, active conservation at Jiankou and a reconstructed component at Yongdingmen.
- End with a four-question field test and a recovery path when labels or records are absent.
- Keep current project status dated and hand off to the official owner instead of freezing completion claims.
