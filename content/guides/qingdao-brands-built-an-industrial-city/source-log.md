## Official and primary evidence

1. [Tsingtao Beer Museum — museum introduction](https://www.tsingtaomuseum.com/category/1773519016732667906) identifies the Dengzhou Road 56 location, 1903 industrial complex, retained machinery/archives and the juxtaposition of heritage galleries with contemporary production. It is first-party museum interpretation.
2. [Qingdao Municipal People's Government — industrial heritage reuse case](https://www.qingdao.gov.cn/ywdt/zwzl/cjzl2026/dxal2026/202607/t20260703_10650841.shtml) is the current municipal framing of the brewery site as industrial-heritage reuse. The page intermittently timed out during direct open; the indexed official result was checked on 2026-08-13. Central should re-open before publication.
3. [Haier Group — development history](https://www.haier.com/about-haier/history/) records the 1984 quality-building phase, the Qingdao Refrigerator General Factory lineage and the 1991 formation of Haier Group after combining other Qingdao appliance factories. These are corporate primary claims, not independent causal analysis.
4. [Haier Group — public learning venues](https://www.haier.com/press-events/interview/) identifies the Haier World Home Appliance Museum proposition at Donghai East Road 52 and provides a contact route. A listed telephone number is not treated as walk-in access.
5. [CRRC Qingdao Sifang — current company profile](https://www.crrcgc.cc/sfgf/36_2134/36_2144/index.html) describes current R&D, testing and manufacturing platforms across high-speed, intercity and urban rail.
6. [CRRC Qingdao Sifang — history](https://www.crrcgc.cc/sfgfen/53_3341/53_4020/index.html) dates the predecessor Sifang Locomotive Works to October 1900 and describes its early repair/assembly role. The English page is useful primary evidence but contains company promotional framing and some dated production-capacity claims; those figures were excluded.

## Independent cross-sources and editorial decisions

1. Shen Hou, [“Nature's Tonic: Beer, Ecology, and Urbanization in a Chinese City, 1900–50”](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1093/envhis/emy136), *Environmental History*, DOI 10.1093/envhis/emy136. This is the independent basis for linking brewery history to water, environmental change and urbanization. The page does not repeat numeric water-use estimates without the article text in view.
2. Jun Yeup Kim and Le-Yin Zhang, [“Formation of Foreign Direct Investment Clustering—A New Path to Local Economic Development? The Case of Qingdao”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00343400601145186), *Regional Studies*, DOI 10.1080/00343400601145186. This supports the distinction between a visible lead firm and the wider local/foreign firm network; it does not establish a current economic share.
3. Quan Guang-Ri, Ryu Ju-Hyun and Sung-Cheol Lee, [“R&D Networks Structure and Spatial Characteristics of Consumer Electronic Industry in Qingdao, China”](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART001679817), *Journal of the Economic Geographical Society of Korea* 15(2), DOI 10.23841/egsk.2012.15.2.292. Its abstract specifically finds a Qingdao appliance cluster centred on large firms while noting weak local R&D collaboration and stronger extra-regional links. This prevents a simplistic “one company built a self-contained city cluster” claim.
