## 1. Hero

An original Homeground editorial illustration has been created at `public/images/guides/how-to-pay-in-china-as-a-tourist/hero-1600.webp` (1600 × 1000). It shows a generic phone, physical card and cash as a layered backup system. It contains no real payment-provider logos, QR code, card number, merchant, traveller or claimed transaction. Localized hero alt text explicitly identifies it as an illustration.

## 2. Phone or cashier photo

No secondary payment photo is required for the copy to work. If a cleared real photo becomes available, one close but non-sensitive phone-and-counter image could support the setup section. Do not make a staged QR-payment scene or use a generic “China street” stock image.

## 3. Official app screenshots

The most useful screenshots would be one official Alipay international-card binding/help screen and one official Weixin Pay international-card or fee notice. They should explain where a traveller sees the supported card, identity prompt or fee—not reproduce a long click-by-click tutorial. Text links to the official pages are sufficient if cleared screenshots are unavailable.

## 4. Brand and screenshot restrictions

Alipay, Alipay+, Weixin Pay, Visa, Mastercard, UnionPay and other marks belong to their respective owners. Use official assets or obtain permission; do not alter logos, hide warnings, expose personal/payment data, or imply Homeground is endorsed by a payment provider. Check the current app-page and brand-use terms before publication.

## 5. Use layout instead of images

The Alipay/Weixin Pay/card/cash comparison, minimum viable setup, 2026 fee-and-limit table, family backup plan and payment-failure recovery flow should remain HTML tables, callouts and short flow cards. They are dynamic and should not be frozen into hard-to-update graphics.

No documentary payment photo has been fabricated. The article uses the clearly described editorial illustration until a properly cleared real photograph becomes available.
