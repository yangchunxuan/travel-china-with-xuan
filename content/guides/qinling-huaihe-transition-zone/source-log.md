## Claim map

| Claim area | Evidence used | Editorial boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Transition-zone concept | CAS Institute page summarises research modelling a western Qinling–Daba and eastern Jianghuai lowland belt, about 1,750 km long and 400 km wide on average. | These are research-model dimensions, not a legal boundary, route guarantee or a single accepted line. |
| Mountain mechanism | CMA and Shaanxi provincial planning material describe Qinling as a climate division, major ecological barrier and watershed; CMA contrasts the Guanzhong and Hanzhong sides. | This does not mean every visible change occurs at the main ridge or that an individual cold-air event stops there. |
| Multiple indicators | CMA lists several familiar boundary associations; transition-zone scholarship treats temperature, precipitation, vegetation, soil and other criteria as multidimensional. | January 0°C and roughly 800 mm precipitation are long-term shorthand. Their isolines move and do not fully coincide with each other or with species and land-use limits. |
| Vegetation overlap | Peer-reviewed transect studies find interlacing temperate and subtropical plant components and boundary disagreement by ridge, slope, latitude and elevation. | A traveller cannot identify an ecological boundary from one tree, landscaped street or plantation. |
| Climate variation | A 2023 *Ecological Indicators* study uses 144 stations and finds different climate-change characteristics across eastern, central and western parts of the zone. | This supports longitudinal and temporal caution; it is not a trip-date forecast. |
| Agricultural landscape | CMA provides the broad wheat/dry-field versus rice/paddy contrast around Qinling. | Irrigation, crop choice, greenhouses, urbanisation and season produce exceptions. This page does not recommend dishes or restaurants. |
