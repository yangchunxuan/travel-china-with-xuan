# Canonical boundary

Reviewed: 2026-09-04

## Owner

This page owns one task: **how a first-time visitor can read Chengdu Wuhou Shrine in a physical sequence while keeping recorded Three Kingdoms history, later memorial construction, literary characterization and present-day tourism presentation separate**.

The canonical decision is not “a biography of Zhuge Liang” and not “everything about the Three Kingdoms.” The information gain is the evidence key attached to an executable museum-forward route: entrance name → Tang stele → Liu Bei Hall and corridors → passage and Zhuge Liang Hall → Sanyi Temple → Huiling → optional Jinli.

## It does not own

- a Chengdu itinerary, transport directions, neighbourhood or accommodation choice;
- a full history of the Three Kingdoms or a Zhuge Liang/Liu Bei biography;
- a chapter-by-chapter retelling of *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*;
- a Jinli food or shopping guide;
- generic museum interpretation or archaeological-site methodology;
- Sichuan opera selection, face-changing, Sanxingdui booking or wider Sichuan routing;
- fixed opening hours, ticket prices, temporary exhibitions or guide inventory.

## Guardrails

- “History” means a claim tied to a historical record or established person/event; it does not make a later statue a portrait.
- “Memorial history” means what later builders, patrons and communities chose to preserve or stage.
- “Literature” includes narrative shaping in *Romance of the Three Kingdoms*; familiar scenes are not automatically recorded events.
- “Tourism presentation” identifies later visitor infrastructure and themed presentation without dismissing it as valueless.
- The page does not call every visible building “Three Kingdoms-era,” every statue a likeness, Huiling excavated proof, or Jinli an ancient Shu-Han street.
- All operations remain date-sensitive and route advice survives temporary closures.
