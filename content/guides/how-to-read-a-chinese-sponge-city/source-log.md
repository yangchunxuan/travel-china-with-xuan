## User task and evidence boundary

The page teaches a traveller to identify an intended stormwater chain from a safe public path after rain. It does **not** perform engineering acceptance, diagnose a single ponding event, promise pollutant removal, certify flood safety or replace official weather warnings.

Evidence is separated as follows:

- National documents establish policy vocabulary, design principles and targets. A target is not treated as a measured nationwide result.
- A local-government project description establishes the intended features at one named public park. It does not establish current maintenance or event-by-event performance.
- Peer-reviewed reviews support the causal limits around rainfall, antecedent wetness, soil, green–grey integration, monitoring, clogging and maintenance.
- Homeground's five-point water-route exercise and traveller scenarios are editorial interpretation built from those sources, not an official inspection method.

## Official and primary sources

1. [State Council guidance on sponge-city construction](https://www.mof.gov.cn/zhengwuxinxi/zhengcefabu/201510/t20151016_1507043.htm) — republished by the Ministry of Finance. Used for the sequence `渗、滞、蓄、净、用、排` and the 70% policy objective. The page explicitly labels it as a target, not proof of attainment.
2. [Technical Guide for Sponge City Construction—Building Low-Impact Development Stormwater Systems (trial)](https://www.mohurd.gov.cn/file/2022/20220801/7f6280a1-540e-4ab9-b519-ac2f9cf0b938.pdf) — Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development. Used for the system frame, facility vocabulary, connection to conventional drainage and the need for construction, operation and maintenance. It is guidance, not a certificate for a photographed facility.
3. [Jinxi Park selected as a Suzhou sponge-city demonstration project](https://www.suzhou.gov.cn/szsrmzf/qxkx/202401/c078b782da5347aa85f765495191572b.shtml) — Suzhou Municipal People's Government. Used only to identify the documented combination of terrain, rain gardens, sunken green space, permeable paving and roof-runoff planters in one public project.
4. [Shanghai implementation opinion on sponge-city construction](https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/nw39225/20200821/0001-39225_45622.html) — Shanghai Municipal People's Government. Used as a cross-check that local implementation integrates parks, wetlands, rain gardens, sunken green space, swales, permeable materials and municipal drainage; Shanghai percentages are not generalized nationally.

## Independent peer-reviewed research

1. [Review of Sponge City implementation in China: performance and policy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38017674/) — *Water Science and Technology*, DOI 10.2166/wst.2023.312. Supports the need to account for variable climate and local conditions rather than claim uniform performance.
2. [Sponge city practice in China: a review of construction, assessment, operational and maintenance](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959652620350071) — *Journal of Cleaner Production*. Supports green–grey integration, monitoring and operation/maintenance boundaries.
3. [Urban pluvial flooding and stormwater management: a contemporary review of China's challenges and “sponge cities” strategy](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1462901117306123) — *Environmental Science & Policy*, DOI 10.1016/j.envsci.2017.11.016. Supports adaptive, integrated implementation rather than treating a visible feature as a complete flood solution.
4. [A review of permeable pavement clogging investigations and recommended maintenance regimes](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/10/3/337) — *Water*, DOI 10.3390/w10030337. Supports the clogging and maintenance limits; no infiltration rate is transferred to a Chinese site.
5. [Evaluating the effectiveness of bioretention cells for urban stormwater management: a systematic review](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/15/5/913) — *Water*, DOI 10.3390/w15050913. Supports rainfall-intensity, antecedent-moisture and media/soil dependencies; no study-specific reduction percentage is used.

## Claim decisions

- Short-lived shallow ponding in a planted basin is described as *potentially intentional*, never automatically successful.
- Persistent ponding is a reportable observation, not a traveller diagnosis of clogging or failure.
- Captured stormwater is never described as potable or clean without measurements.
- The article gives no universal drainage time, storm return period, infiltration rate, pollutant-removal rate or citywide protection level.
- The page does not use the 2015 policy's staged built-area goals as evidence that they have been achieved by 2026.
