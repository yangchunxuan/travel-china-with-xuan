### Why this image, and why this particular frame

The article's central practical claim is that Guangji Bridge is sometimes not a crossing at all,
because its middle section is a pontoon that closes in the morning and opens in the evening. Most
photographs of the bridge show it closed, which would quietly contradict the page. **This one shows
it open**: the beam spans reach out from the bank and then stop, there is clear water in the middle,
and the boats that form the movable section are moored aside in a red line. A reader who looks at
the header before reading a word already understands the decision the article asks them to make.

The 17:49 capture time is consistent with the evening opening described in the sources. That is a
useful coincidence and is **not** offered in the body as evidence of the schedule; the schedule is
sourced from the provincial reporting, not from the photograph.

### Crop review

- **Desktop (wide):** the bridge runs across the lower third with the gap near the centre, so the
  open section is the first thing the eye lands on. Sky fills the upper half and carries the
  headline overlay cleanly.
- **Mobile (390 px):** the shared template renders the same 1.6:1 ratio, so the composition is
  unchanged; the gap stays near the horizontal centre and does not fall into the outer 8% that a
  narrow viewport can clip.

### Age of the photograph

Taken February 2023. It shows the bridge's **configuration**, which is a design feature rather than
a current operating state, and the body sources the schedule separately and tells the reader to
confirm on the day. Nothing in the text depends on the image's date.
