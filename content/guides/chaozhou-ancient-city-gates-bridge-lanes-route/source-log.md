## 1. Guangdong Provincial Local Chronicles Office — the Chaozhou city wall: **VERIFIED, read in full**

- **Institution:** 广东省人民政府地方志办公室 (Guangdong Provincial People's Government Local
  Chronicles Office) with 潮州市地方志编纂委员会办公室 (Chaozhou Municipal Local Chronicles
  Compilation Committee Office), credited jointly on the page
- **Page title:** 诗意潮州｜潮州古城墙
- **URL:** http://dfz.gd.gov.cn/zjgd/content/post_3058024.html
- **Published:** 2020-08-03 · **Retrieved:** 2026-08-22 · **Rechecked:** 2026-08-23
- **Supports:**
  - "三山一水护城廓"; only the **east-facing wall** survives, beginning at 金山 at the north end and
    running south to 南门, where it joins the south dyke.
  - Construction: stone courses at the base, interlocking blue brick in the body.
  - Chronology: at least back to the Tang as rammed earth; brick replacing earth from the Song;
    **a circuit of about 5.3 km by the Southern Song with as many as 11 gates**; stone-built under
    the Ming; further repaired under the Qing; extent barely changed from Song to Qing; **in the
    early Republic the walls were demolished to build roads and other municipal works, leaving only
    the riverside wall.**
  - The wall is **also a flood dyke**. The 1871 flood came through it and boats sailed in the
    streets; afterwards the east wall's foundation was deep-excavated and rebuilt with a rammed
    lime-and-sand core and stone facing.
  - **When the river rises, boards are dropped into the gate openings and sandbags are stacked**,
    so the defensive wall becomes a water barrier. This city-wall-as-dyke survives in only a
    handful of places nationally, **with Chaozhou as the type example, still performing the role.**
  - **The east wall has four gates**; 广济门 (the East Gate), directly facing Guangji Bridge, is the
    grandest — **three storeys, five bays wide, one of the few surviving Ming gate towers.**
    下水门 is separately named and photographed on the page.
- **Cannot support:** the names of all four gates (it names two); any opening time; any ticket;
  anything about the bridge's operation; anything about the lanes.
- **Dynamic:** low. This is settled historical and structural material.

## 2. Guangdong Overseas Chinese Affairs Office — Guangji Bridge: **VERIFIED, read in full**

- **Institution:** 广东省人民政府侨务办公室 (Overseas Chinese Affairs Office of the People's
  Government of Guangdong Province), carrying a 羊城晚报 report that quotes **潮州市古城文物保护管理
  中心** (Chaozhou Municipal Ancient City Cultural Relics Protection and Management Centre) staff by
  name and title
- **Page title:** 潮州广济桥：千年古桥见证历史，饱经岁月风采更盛
- **URL:** http://www.qb.gd.gov.cn/jrqx/content/post_1250040.html
- **Published:** 2024-07-03 · **Retrieved:** 2026-08-22 · **Rechecked:** 2026-08-23
- **Supports:**
  - Founded **Southern Song Qiandao 7 (1171)** as 康济桥 — one stone pier plus 86 shuttle boats
    chained into a pontoon.
  - Successive officials extended piers from both banks and reduced the boats; **Ming Xuande 10
    (1435)** major rebuild with pavilions and 24 boats midstream, renamed 广济桥; **Jiajing 9
    (1530)** reduced to 18 boats, producing "十八梭船廿四洲".
  - **The bridge combines beam, arch and pontoon forms.** The 18-boat pontoon section
    **closes in the morning and opens in the evening**; Mao Yisheng called it the world's earliest
    opening-and-closing bridge. "洲" refers to the piers.
  - 2003 municipal restoration completed **2007**, restoring the Ming appearance, 12 pavilions and
    18 kiosks, and **fixing the bridge's role as a pedestrian sightseeing bridge**.
  - Historic character: pavilions doubled as shops; "一里长桥一里市".
- **Cannot support:** current opening hours, ticket prices, the light-show calendar, or whether the
  pontoon will be open on a given day. **None of these appears in the body.**
- **Dynamic:** the morning-close / evening-open pattern is an operating practice and is treated as
  such — the body tells the reader to confirm on the day and to go early rather than at dusk.

## 3. Guangdong Overseas Chinese Affairs Office — Chaozhou Ancient City: **VERIFIED, read in full**

- **Institution:** same office, carrying a 潮州日报 report quoting the local historian 陈贤武
- **Page title:** 潮州古城：人文荟萃冠岭东 烟火最是故乡浓
- **URL:** http://www.qb.gd.gov.cn/mlgd/content/post_981941.html
- **Published:** 2022-06-24 · **Retrieved:** 2026-08-22 · **Rechecked:** 2026-08-23
- **Supports:**
  - 广济门城楼 is the city's landmark, its first-floor eaves carrying the plaque 岭东首邑.
  - The old city **still holds its Song-era street-block pattern**.
  - **Quarter layout:** administrative in the north, residential in the south, commercial in the
    east, handicraft and common housing in the west, arranged on a deliberate scheme.
  - **太平路 is the north-south central axis** of the old city, with **23 stone memorial arches**
    along it; named examples include 十相留声·太山北斗坊, 状元坊 and 戊辰八贤·盛世元凯坊.
  - Old wells including 义井 sit around the arch street.
  - **The ten named lanes:** 猷、灶、义、兴、甲、家、石、辜、郑、庵 — the most representative
    traditional dwelling clusters, with clan inscriptions above doorways.
  - 潮州老城古民居建筑群 is a **national key cultural relic protection unit**; 牌坊街 has been rated
    a national-level tourism leisure block.
  - Professor 陈平原 describes Chaozhou as a **"living" ancient city**, not commercialised or
    museum-ified, whose most vivid feature is residents' plain, unhurried everyday life.
  - The local greeting "坐，食茶".
- **Cannot support:** the gate names; any opening time; the bridge's operation.
- **Dynamic:** low for structure; the leisure-block rating is an award and could change, so it is
  **not** stated in the body.

## 4. Gate names — corroborated, not directly verified

The four east-wall gates are named in the body as **上水门, 竹木门, 广济门 and 下水门**. Source 1,
read in full, establishes that the east wall has exactly four gates and names **广济门** and
**下水门**. The remaining two names, **上水门** and **竹木门**, come from:

- consistent naming in indexed government pages that could not be opened
  (`zfcxjst.gd.gov.cn`, `chaozhou.gov.cn`, `xiangqiao.gov.cn`), and
- first-party photographs on Wikimedia Commons of gates physically signed 上水门 and 竹木门 in
  Chaozhou, uploaded 2025-09-30.

That is corroboration, not verification, and it is flagged here and in the body's review callout.
The etymological reading of the names — upper and lower water gates upstream and downstream, and a
bamboo-and-timber gate named for its trade — is presented in the body as what the names describe,
supported by the same indexed material, and is not load-bearing for any route instruction.
