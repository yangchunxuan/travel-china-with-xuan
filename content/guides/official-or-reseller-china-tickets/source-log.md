## Editorial and parity review

- English is the authoritative factual baseline and is within the 900–1,500-word production range.
- Chinese and Korean preserve all channel checks, three scenarios, booking/refund states, privacy rules, live-policy boundaries and final verification steps.
- “Official” means named or linked by the attraction owner. Search position, advertising, a verified social badge, price or the seller’s own “official” claim are not treated as proof.
- Payment, an order number and a reseller voucher are separated from owner-confirmed, document-matched admission.
- A group portal available to registered travel agencies is not treated as authorisation to resell standalone tickets on a public marketplace.

## Dynamic conflicts and limits

- The Palace Museum page surfaced an obsolete epidemic sentence alongside current personal-ticket rules and 2026 notices. No obsolete health rule is used. Release times, accepted documents and cancellation rules must be checked in the current official mini program at checkout.
- Mogao’s 2026 visitor notice allows registered, reviewed travel-agency users to book through the owner’s official system; the newer 18 July 2026 statement says no third party is authorised to act as a ticket agent. The guide resolves this conservatively: owner-controlled agency access is not public standalone-ticket resale authority.
- The Qinshihuang museum’s official site links to a separate ticket technology domain. This is an example of a valid link chain, not a rule that matching domains are required.
- Trip.com terms vary by locale and product. They support only the general example that a reseller order can remain subject to supplier availability, verification and product-specific terms.
