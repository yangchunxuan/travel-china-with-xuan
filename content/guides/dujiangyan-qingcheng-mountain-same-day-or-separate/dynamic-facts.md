## Stable facts used in the decision

- UNESCO inscribed Mount Qingcheng and the Dujiangyan Irrigation System together in 2000.
- UNESCO describes Dujiangyan as a water-management system that continues to serve the Chengdu Plain and identifies Yuzui, Feishayan and Baopingkou as its three key works.
- UNESCO associates Mount Qingcheng with the development of Daoism and a mountain temple landscape.
- The shared inscription explains a cultural relationship; it does not create one gate, ticket, route or operating schedule.
- Dujiangyan Irrigation System and Qingcheng Front Mountain require a geographic transfer and different on-site movement.

## Homeground editorial judgements

The following are planning mechanisms, not official rules:

- combine the sites only when “Mount Qingcheng” means a shortened Front Mountain visit;
- give one site anchor status and define the second site's compact core before departure;
- Dujiangyan's minimum coherent visit connects Yuzui, Feishayan and Baopingkou;
- Front Mountain's minimum coherent visit includes a wooded path transition, one named religious place and a safe turning point;
- choose Dujiangyan alone for engineering, a more bounded day or reduced stair exposure;
- choose Front Mountain alone when the mountain-and-Daoist-temple landscape is the real purpose;
- split the sites for slower walkers, architecture readers, photographers, unsettled weather or when both are priorities;
- cut Back Mountain first, then optional upper targets or peripheral town time;
- a single-site day is the designed recovery, not a failed version of the plan.
