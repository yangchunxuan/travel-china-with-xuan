## Front Mountain, Back Mountain and the UNESCO boundary

The one-day combination in this page uses Front Mountain as a **visitor task**, because its path-and-temple purpose is the relevant contrast with Dujiangyan's engineering purpose. A dated 9 June 2026 visitor-information item names Front Mountain and Back Mountain separately, while a 2025 official plan treats them as distinct planning areas. The live ticket portal reviewed for this draft lists Dujiangyan and Front Mountain, not Back Mountain; the dated item and plan are therefore not used as proof of current Back Mountain admission or operation.

That contemporary visitor-management distinction must not be converted into a claim about the legal or technical boundary of the UNESCO property. UNESCO's property documents, map record and 2023 periodic report contain boundary and buffer-zone information that requires specialist reading. This article therefore does not say that all of Front Mountain is inside, that Back Mountain is outside, or that the modern ticket boundary equals the World Heritage boundary. It says only that UNESCO inscribed Mount Qingcheng and the Dujiangyan Irrigation System together and that the combined-day decision discussed here concerns the Front Mountain visitor experience.
