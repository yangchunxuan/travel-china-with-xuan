## Editorial boundary

This page owns the route-level method for verifying a wheelchair access chain across transport, station or airport handoffs, last-mile vehicles, hotels, toilets, venues and failure recovery. It does not certify any city or facility, promise that a lift is working, prescribe an individual transfer technique, or replace medical, occupational-therapy or personal-care advice.

The article deliberately separates three types of evidence:

1. **Legal or standards duty:** what authorities and operators are required or encouraged to provide.
2. **Published facility or service information:** what a named operator says exists.
3. **Traveller-specific operating confirmation:** whether the exact date, chair, transfer method, vehicle, room and route work together.

A fact at level 1 or 2 is not treated as proof of level 3.

### 1. China Disabled Persons' Federation and transport authorities — joint accessible-transport notice

- URL: https://www.cdpf.org.cn/zwgk/zcwj/wjfb/e08521a0f1e741c6a2e77e4b43500796.htm
- Publisher: Ministry of Transport, National Railway Administration, CAAC, China Disabled Persons' Federation and other authorities
- Published: 2024-01-15 (document dated 2024-01-12)
- Checked: 2026-08-13
- Facts used: New or altered transport infrastructure should follow accessibility standards and connect with surrounding accessible facilities; existing transport that cannot be retrofitted should use necessary alternative measures; operators are directed to maintain lifts, assistance phones, boarding plates and other equipment and respond to assistance requests.
- Boundary: This is a policy and service framework. It does not demonstrate implementation at a particular station, stop or vehicle on a travel date.
- Update trigger: Replacement notice; revised transport accessibility standard; operator-level process change.

### 6. CAAC — Measures for the Air Transport of Persons with Disabilities

- URL: https://www.caac.gov.cn/XXGK/XXGK/ZFGW/201601/t20160122_27671.html
- Publisher: Civil Aviation Administration of China
- Status shown by publisher: Effective
- Checked: 2026-08-13
- Facts used: For the services listed in the rules—including checking a powered wheelchair, using an onboard aisle chair, medical oxygen and travelling with a service dog—the request must currently be made at booking and no later than 48 hours before departure. Passengers needing boarding or disembarkation help should complete check-in 2 hours before the ordinary deadline. The carrier should provide information about chair storage, aircraft accessibility and onboard toilets; the rules also address door-side chair use and return, assistance when no bridge is available, irregular-flight support and assistive-device handling.
- Boundary: Aircraft type, aisle-chair availability, battery acceptance, transfer method and remote-stand operation remain booking-specific. The article does not infer that all aircraft toilets are wheelchair accessible.
- Update trigger: Replacement regulation; carrier special-assistance process; dangerous-goods or battery rule change.

## Homeground editorial judgments

- The weakest essential link decides whether the segment is “go,” “conditional” or “redesign.”
- A conditional non-critical link is acceptable only when a named and obtainable fallback has been checked against the traveller's own requirements. An essential link that remains incompatible or unverified requires redesign.
- Direct transport can be preferable even when a connection appears accessible, because each handoff adds another failure point.
- Fewer cities can produce more usable sightseeing time when they remove repeated hotel moves and transfer verification.
- A companion's presence does not convert unsafe lifting, an incompatible bathroom or a missing lift into an acceptable plan.
- Toilets are route infrastructure, not an optional amenity.
