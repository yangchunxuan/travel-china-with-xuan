## Asset principle

This page must not use an AI-generated documentary scene, a generic wheelchair stock photograph or a single ramp as evidence that a route is accessible. Planning imagery should explain the **continuity of the chain**. Any photograph that depicts a real facility must have traceable permission and must be captioned narrowly; it cannot certify the wider station, hotel, venue or city.

## Hero — original access-chain information graphic

- Required output: `public/images/guides/wheelchair-accessible-china-route-planning/hero-1600.webp`
- Canvas: 1600 × 1000 px, WebP.
- Preferred source: original Homeground vector illustration retained as an editable SVG in the article directory.
- Concept: one continuous line linking eight clearly different nodes — arrival, intercity transport, exit, last mile, hotel, toilet, venue and fallback. Include one deliberate broken link with a safe alternate branch.
- Human representation: simple editorial figure using a wheelchair; no photorealism, no identifiable person and no implication that the illustrated equipment exists at a named facility.
- Avoid: national accessibility score, green “approved” stamp, city ranking, copied map tiles, airline/rail logos, medical imagery or staff lifting a traveller.
- Three-language crop: the hero should work without embedded prose. If labels are necessary, create separately editable EN/ZH/KO exports with identical meaning.
- Alt-text boundary: describe it as an editorial access-chain diagram, not documentary evidence.

## Inline asset 1 — real transport handoff

- Scene: a real, staffed wheelchair assistance handoff at a named Chinese railway station or airport, ideally showing the transition between curb/terminal, service point and onward route.
- Source priority: operator press office, government media library or a Homeground-commissioned photograph with written permission and consent from any identifiable traveller.
- Caption must name only what is visible and verified. Example boundary: “A staffed assistance point at [named terminal], photographed on [date]. Service must still be reserved and reconfirmed.”
- Do not use a photo of a station wheelchair to imply that the booked train, aircraft or destination vehicle is suitable.

## Inline asset 2 — original verification worksheet

- Format: editorial diagram or printable card, not a screenshot of a provider app.
- Content: five measurement groups — chair, door, bed, bathroom and vehicle — plus confirmation owner, date checked and fallback.
- Purpose: turn the article's evidence ladder into a reusable verification aid without publishing a personalised route.
- Accessibility: strong contrast, large labels, no colour-only status cues; pair icons with text.

## Inline asset 3 — real room-detail evidence, optional

- Scene: a commissioned set showing an actual accessible-room doorway, approach to bed, toilet transfer side and shower threshold with a measuring tape.
- Rights: property permission plus photographer licence recorded in the repository.
- Boundary: caption the exact room category and photography date; state that availability and traveller fit still require direct confirmation.
- Do not use a generic hotel bathroom or an image where measurements cannot be read.
