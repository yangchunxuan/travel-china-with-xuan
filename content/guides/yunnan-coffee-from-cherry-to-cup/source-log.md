## Editorial boundary

This page follows a coffee lot from cherry, place and variety through picking, reception, primary processing, drying, hulling and sorting, green-bean trade, roasting and brewing. It explains how Yunnan is retaining more identity and processing value without assigning one flavour to the province.

It is not a café or brand ranking, estate itinerary, current-price page, product-stock page or promise that a farm or processing facility accepts visitors. `精品率` is treated as an official aggregate term, not translated into a consumer specialty guarantee. Policy targets, reported outcomes, product claims and Homeground verification advice are kept separate.

## Statistical conflict ledger

The 2025 figures are not presented as a clean time series:

- January 2026 agriculture-department release: `31.6% / 80% / 146.3万亩`.
- July 2026 provincial account: `41.7% / 85%`; the currently visible page reviewed on 2026-08-13 renders `146.3万亩`.
The sources do not expose a shared denominator, exact category method, cut-off date or revision note sufficient to reconcile the percentage figures. The copy therefore records both percentage versions, uses only the direction of change, and avoids calculating growth from the conflicting values. It does not retain an acreage variant that lacks a citable archived page.

## Independent research cross-checks

1. [Influence of Different Primary Process on the Quality of Arabica Coffee in Yunnan Province](https://doi.org/10.13982/j.mfst.1673-9078.2019.2.021), *Modern Food Science and Technology* (2019). A controlled comparison of six primary processes using ripe fruit from a Yunnan Catimor-series source supports the narrow claim that processing can change chemical and sensory outcomes. It does not define one flavour for each process.
2. [Using farmers’ local knowledge of tree provision of ecosystem services to strengthen the emergence of coffee-agroforestry landscapes in southwest China](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0204046), *PLOS ONE* (2018). Farm inventories and interviews in Pu’er and Xishuangbanna support the point that shade-tree choices involve locally perceived services and disservices rather than one universal prescription.
3. [Exploring the chemistry and craft behind coffee flavour underpinned by the growing coffee industry in China (Yunnan): A review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfca.2026.108886), *Journal of Food Composition and Analysis* (2026). Used as a current research map for the interaction among variety, processing, roasting and extraction; not used as proof of industry totals.

## Fact and judgment separation

- **Official fact:** named policy targets, dated reported industry figures, current standard record, and officially described processing infrastructure.
- **Research finding:** primary process affects measured composition and sensory results; farmers report both benefits and drawbacks from shade trees.
- **Editorial judgment:** the nine-stage trace, strong/weak label comparison, verification sequence and recovery decisions.
- **Not claimed:** one Yunnan flavour, universal use of a named method, legal compliance by an uninspected seller, current estate access, current product availability, price or farmer-income outcome.
