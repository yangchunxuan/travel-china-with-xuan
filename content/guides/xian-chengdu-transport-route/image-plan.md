### Why this image and not a skyline

The guide's whole argument is that the *hub*, not the city, decides the day. A Xi'an skyline would
illustrate nothing. This frame shows the specific object the reader has to navigate: a very large
concourse, overhead train information boards, lettered gates and travellers moving with wheeled
luggage — which is exactly the "walk the concourse, read the full station name, allow for security"
material in the body.

### Licence and provenance checks performed

- Licence read from the Commons file page's own `extmetadata`, not inferred from a search
  thumbnail or a reverse-image result.
- Author is stated as the uploader with "Own work" as the credit, so attribution is unambiguous.
- Attribution is carried in three places: the `heroCredit` block of every locale in
  `metadata.json`, the sources block at the foot of each language body, and this file.
- The image is a photograph. No AI-generated image is used anywhere in this guide.

### Privacy and content checks

- No identity documents, bank cards, boarding passes, tickets, order screens or telephone numbers
  are legible anywhere in the frame.
- The crop was chosen specifically to move the closest foreground figures towards the lower edge.
  The people who remain are at small scale, in motion, in a public transport concourse, and are
  incidental to the subject rather than the subject.
- The visible information boards carry train and gate information only.

### Crop review

- **Desktop (wide):** the roof geometry leads to the centre; the information boards sit on the
  lower third and stay legible as texture rather than as readable data.
- **Mobile (390 px):** the shared template renders the hero at the same 1.6:1 ratio, so the
  composition holds; the boards remain in frame and nothing important sits in the outer 8% that a
  narrow viewport tends to clip.
