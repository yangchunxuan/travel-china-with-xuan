# Dynamic facts — beijing-copper-pot-mutton-ordering-workflow

Reviewed 2026-08-22, Asia/Taipei.

This topic looks static and mostly is. The risk here is the opposite of a live-status page: the
temptation is to dress up a **restaurant-specific** or **retail-specific** fact as a permanent
national rule. The table below separates the four kinds of statement the brief asks to be kept
apart, and then lists what actually needs re-checking.

## High-volatility facts deliberately excluded from the body

- **Every price**, including per-plate prices, set menus and service charges.
- **Every opening hour, branch address, queue length and booking channel.**
- **Any statement that a named house is currently good, authentic or worth travelling for.**
  Houses appear only as documented methods.
- **Any cooking time in seconds.** This is the one exclusion the article argues for out loud,
  because the invented figure is both the most repeated claim on this topic and the only one that
  could plausibly cause harm.
- **Any allergen, halal-certification or dietary guarantee.**
- **Any claim about what mutton costs, where it comes from today, or how a given plate was
  handled.** The article converts all of these into questions the reader asks at the table.

## Freshness note for central

Rows 1, 6 and 7 are the ones to watch. Row 1 is a regulatory gap that could close at any time and
would change the advice. Rows 6 and 7 are health statements; if the issuing bodies revise them, the
safety callout and the closing paragraph must move together in all three languages.

Rows 9 and 10 are the ones most likely to be quietly wrong later: heritage lists get supplemented
and brand registers get revised. They are low-consequence individually, but they are the article's
factual backbone for the "four documented versions" table, so a reviewer who finds one stale should
check all four rows rather than one.

**Nothing in this article should ever be updated by adding a restaurant recommendation, a price or a
timing in seconds.** If a future editor feels the pull, the correct response is to strengthen the
question-asking language instead.
