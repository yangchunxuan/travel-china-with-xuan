# Canonical boundary — xian-yangrou-paomo-first-bowl

## Owner

This page owns the table-level task that begins when a traveller decides to order one Xi'an
`yangrou paomo` or beef paomo: identify the meat and broth, choose the shop's available bread
service and soup style, break an appropriate amount, hand the bowl back, use accompaniments, and
recover from a mismatched order.

## It does not own

- a ranking of paomo restaurants, Muslim Quarter stalls or Xi'an food streets;
- a general list of Xi'an dishes or a city itinerary;
- a recipe, kitchen production standard or test of which shop is “most authentic”;
- a general guide to Hui people, Islam in China or halal certification;
- all dishes called `paomo`, including hulutou paomo, douhua paomo, xiaochao paomo and regional
  mutton-soup formats;
- current prices, opening hours, queue estimates, branch lists or delivery availability.

## Evidence boundary

The official heritage records describe named lineages: Lao Sun Jia, Tong Sheng Xiang and Lao Liu
Jia. Their pea-sized bread, one-bowl cooking, style names and eating descriptions establish
documented practices, not compulsory behaviour in every 2026 restaurant. The 2016 city technical
rules were expressly presented as recommendatory rather than mandatory. Shop service therefore
uses “ask”, “if offered” and “at the documented lineage” language.

Ancient lamb-soup, Silk Road and imperial-meal stories appear in heritage and brand narratives.
They are not treated as proof that today's bread-and-broth dish existed in its current form in the
Zhou, Han or Tang periods. Secure history begins with named modern lineages and official listing
records.
