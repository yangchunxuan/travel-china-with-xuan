## Research question

How can a first-time visitor use the China National Silk Museum to follow silk from living sericulture to a woven object and then to conservation, while keeping material, technique, object history, reconstruction, exchange evidence and current visitor operations separate?

## Search and duplicate audit

Current Google results and visible People Also Ask questions were reviewed on 2026-09-04. Commercial summaries and review platforms frequently answered “what to see” with a gallery list, repeated stale reservation advice, or shifted into silk shopping. Recent traveler discussions helped identify confusion between the museum and retail stops and demand for a short, intelligible route. Those sources are query evidence only; none supports a historical, conservation or operational claim.

The exact title/slug, museum entity and cocoon-to-conservation task were searched on latest `origin/main` and every fetched `origin/article/*` and `origin/codex/*` ref. No competing owner was found. ## Editorial route and traveler value

The order Sericulture → Weaving → Silk Road → Conservation is a Homeground editorial route. It moves from living/material process to structure, then to historical evidence and finally to the limits of preservation. The museum may publish or enforce a different circulation pattern on the day; visitors must follow current signs and staff.

The four-field object test, route variants and closure recovery table are editorial tools. They do not claim museum endorsement. More than half of the body addresses onsite attention, route choice, label reading, operational recovery and fit/skip decisions.
