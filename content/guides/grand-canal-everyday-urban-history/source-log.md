## Evidence used

1. [The Grand Canal](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1443/) — UNESCO World Heritage Centre. Defines the serial World Heritage property; supports the warning that not every waterside place is an inscribed component.
2. [Northern Jiangsu canal freight reporting](https://www.mot.gov.cn/xinwen/jiaotongyaowen/202601/t20260113_4197211.html) — Ministry of Transport of China, 2026. Current evidence that parts of the canal remain working transport infrastructure.
3. [Hangzhou Grand Canal core-zone spatial controls](https://www.hangzhou.gov.cn/art/2023/2/6/art_1229063387_1829308.html) — Hangzhou Municipal Government. Official planning and protection boundaries; not treated as proof of daily venue operations.
4. [Location, Urban Fabric and Transportation](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/edit/10.4324/9780429244605-38/location-urban-fabric-transportation-fang-wang-bingyu-lin-qingyin-liu) — Routledge. Independent urban-form context.
5. [Heritage Modernity: the Grand Canal and everyday life in Hangzhou](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/7jj0h5hn) — University of California eScholarship. Independent scholarship used to frame living heritage without turning residents into spectacle.

## Fact classes and recheck

- Official fact: the World Heritage property is a serial property composed of selected canal sections and heritage elements.
- Current fact: northern Jiangsu canal reaches still serve freight transport; reviewed 2026-08-13.
- Long-term pattern: canals influence crossings, street orientation, land use and exchange. This is analytical context, not a promise about any one street.
- Homeground judgement: choose one section where heritage, working infrastructure and present-day urban life can be read together.
- Recheck before publication: exact public access, construction/flood-control notices, museum/boat hours and whether any selected viewpoint is lawful. No fixed opening hours or ticket prices are frozen in copy.
