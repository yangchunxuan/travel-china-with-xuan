## Safety and editorial boundary

This is a traveller-usability workflow, not professional fire inspection, legal advice or a substitute for on-site fire-rescue direction. The copy deliberately does not instruct a traveller to test an alarm, extinguisher, sprinkler, smoke detector, fire door or restricted exit. During an active alarm, smoke or fire, life-safety action and official direction replace the booking-recovery workflow.

The text separates:

1. operator and authority duties stated in law and standards;
2. what a guest can observe from the assigned room without interfering with equipment; and
3. what must be decided from current smoke, heat, staff and fire-rescue direction during an incident.

## Depth and scenario audit

- Independent actionable information objects: **30**.
- Distinct structure: timed five-minute check → night set-up → active-smoke decision → mobility/sensory/family variants → pre-incident fault recovery → privacy boundary.
- Scenarios: night alarm with smoke outside; wheelchair user above ground level; deaf solo guest; family with children or older relatives.
- Recovery before an emergency: define the exact fault → duty-manager inspection → walk the cleared route → inspect a replacement room's route → relocate if no route works → preserve narrow, redacted evidence after safety is restored.
- Active incident rule: call 119 first; when safe, notify hotel staff; follow fire-rescue and current
  on-site emergency direction. Do not wait for platform support, film evidence, collect luggage or
  return.

## Dynamic maintenance

Recheck the Fire Protection Law and the two current standards at least every six months or after an amendment/replacement notice. Hotel route usability is more volatile than the law: renovation, stored items, room moves, equipment faults and shift procedures can change it on the day. The article tells readers to repeat the five-minute check after every room move.
