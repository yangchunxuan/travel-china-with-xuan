All photographs must be real, licensed for Homeground's intended use, and traceable to a source record. Do not use AI-generated airport or traveller scenes. Do not show a readable passport, boarding pass, booking reference, phone number or identifiable child's face.

## Required hero

- File: `public/images/guides/china-open-jaw-flights-route-planning/hero-1600.webp`
- Size: 1600 × 1000 px, WebP, landscape.
- Scene: international travellers with ordinary luggage checking a large departures board or airport wayfinding inside a recognisably modern Chinese gateway airport.
- Editorial purpose: communicates “arrival city versus departure city” without turning the article into a destination postcard.
- Composition: leave calm space around the upper-left/centre for responsive crops; keep signage legible enough to read as airport information but avoid making a specific live flight the factual focus.
- Preferred sources: an airport authority or airline media library with explicit editorial/commercial permission; otherwise a reputable licensed stock library with model releases where required.
- Avoid: staged passport close-ups, visible PNR/QR codes, immigration counters, security screening, branded fare screenshots, generic skyline montages and AI composites.

## Optional supporting photographs

1. `airport-wayfinding-1200.webp` — 1200 × 800 px. A real bilingual airport wayfinding scene showing that the exact airport and terminal matter. Prefer an official airport-authority image.
2. `airport-rail-transfer-1200.webp` — 1200 × 800 px. Travellers using an airport rail link or a clearly signed interchange, illustrating the hidden transfer inside a “cheap” same-city return.
3. `travellers-luggage-1200.webp` — 1200 × 800 px. A small adult group or multigenerational party moving luggage through a station or airport; faces may be unidentifiable. Use only if it adds real traveller-limit context.

The copy does not depend on the optional images. Add figure blocks only after files, licences, captions and exact dimensions are verified.

## Graphic treatment

Do not commission a route map that readers could mistake for a recommended itinerary. The comparison tables in the shared template should carry the decision framework. If editorial later wants a graphic, use a neutral “linear route versus return-to-start route” diagram with no city sequence, price promise or travel-time claim.
