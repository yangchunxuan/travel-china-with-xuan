# Image plan — how-guangzhou-morning-tea-works

Status: INTEGRATION REVIEW READY

## 1. Hero

Use one real, licensed documentary photograph made in a functioning Guangzhou teahouse or restaurant during morning-tea service. The useful frame is a lived-in table: a teapot and cups, two or three recognisable Cantonese dim sum dishes, steam or service movement, and enough of the room to establish a public social setting. It should not look like an isolated studio food shot. Avoid readable payment QR codes, private order details and identifiable faces without consent. Target: 1600 × 1000 WebP with photographer, venue, capture date and usage-rights notes.

## 2. Supporting food image

One optional real close-up could label a small set of menu anchors—har gow, siu mai, cheung fun or lo mai gai—only when the dishes have been identified by the venue or an experienced editor. Do not use a generic dumpling image and call it Guangzhou dim sum. Record ingredients because visually similar items may contain different meat, shellfish or sauces.

## 3. Service and contemporary practice

If a second documentary scene is available, prefer a real ordering moment: a paper checklist, stamped dim sum card, current QR menu or server presenting steamers. The article explains that these systems coexist; one photograph must not be captioned as the universal Guangzhou method. Blur personal information and obtain the restaurant's permission where required.

## 4. Heritage and official-material options

An official photograph of a Guangfu tea-drinking customs display at a Guangzhou public cultural venue could help explain heritage status, but only with explicit reuse rights and the institution's required credit. A screenshot of the 2026 Guangzhou morning-tea protection regulation has limited visual value; link the live government page instead unless an editor needs a dated evidence inset.

## 5. Rights and representation

Restaurant names, menus, interiors and logos may carry separate permissions. Do not imply that Homeground certifies a venue or that a pictured restaurant holds the voluntary "Guangzhou Traditional Morning Tea Shop" designation unless the current official register confirms it. Do not use AI-generated food, diners, chefs, streets or teahouse interiors as documentary imagery.

## 6. Keep dynamic explanations in HTML

The term comparison, historical timeline, 2026 regulation summary, dish-and-allergen table, etiquette evidence levels and first-visit flow should remain HTML tables, callouts and lists. They need editorial updates and should not be frozen into infographic images.

The selected hero is a real table at Pan Xi Restaurant in Liwan, Guangzhou; no supporting image was added merely to increase image count.
